These funds, which typically hold equities and sell call options, produce taxable income in various forms—dividends, capital gains, or non-dividend distributions—which many investors fail to anticipate until receiving their annual tax documents. Return of capital distributions, while offering short-term tax deferral by reducing the investment's cost basis, ultimately result in a higher capital gains tax liability when shares are sold. Financial advisors strongly recommend holding these ETFs in tax-deferred accounts and meticulously assessing a fund's tax-cost ratio and Net Asset Value growth to accurately gauge after-tax returns.
