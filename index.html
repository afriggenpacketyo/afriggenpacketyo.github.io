Thousands of low-income Medi-Cal patients, many grappling with serious illnesses, are now facing significant challenges in finding new doctors after UCLA Health's long-standing contract with Health Care LA expired. This abrupt change impacts approximately 9,000 patients who previously relied on UCLA for essential specialty care, including critical cancer treatments and major organ transplants. While a tentative agreement has been reached to allow established patients to continue receiving care for another year, UCLA explicitly declined to renegotiate the full contract, citing "capacity issues" at its facilities. Critics, including some UCLA medical professionals, are accusing the public university of prioritizing profits over patient needs, especially noting its expansion into wealthier areas while providing a comparatively lower percentage of Medi-Cal care than other UC health systems.

Opinion: Trump's Proposed Triumphal Arch Criticized as Costly Vanity Project

2026-09-05 06:00:00 EDT

This opinion piece sharply criticizes President Trump's proposed "Great Triumphal Arch" in Washington D.C., slated to be a colossal 250 feet tall and positioned to obscure views of Arlington National Cemetery. Interior Secretary Doug Burgum announced that excavation work would commence soon, framing the project as a grand monument honoring history. However, the article dismisses it as a "vanity project," drawing unfavorable comparisons to those commissioned by authoritarian leaders like Saddam Hussein and Kim Il Sung. Estimated to cost between $100-250 million, funded by a mix of taxpayer money and private donations, the arch is intended for completion before Trump's term ends. Critics highlight the lack of necessary congressional approval and uncompleted FAA reviews, portraying the structure as a costly, intrusive symbol of Trump's ego rather than a genuine triumph.

Researchers Capture First Northeast Pacific Sighting of Elusive Bigfin Squid

2026-09-05 06:00:00 EDT

Researchers embarked on a deep-sea expedition off Monterey, California, have successfully captured the first live footage of the incredibly elusive bigfin squid (Magnapinna) in the northeast Pacific Ocean. This rare sighting, representing one of only approximately 70 ever observed globally, occurred two miles deep as an underwater vehicle filmed the mysterious, pinkish creature with its distinctive "elbow" and languidly undulating fins. The unexpected discovery, made during a mission primarily focused on studying biodiversity in seamounts, powerfully underscores the numerous mysteries still contained within the deep ocean. Scientists will now meticulously analyze collected samples and data to gain further insights into this creature and other deep-sea marine life, reinforcing the vast unknown that characterizes ocean depths.
