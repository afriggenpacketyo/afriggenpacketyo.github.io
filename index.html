Recent appointments to a critical Pentagon advisory body by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth are sparking concerns over potential conflicts of interest, particularly regarding ties to Donald Trump Jr.'s venture capital fund, 1789 Capital. Appointees Marc Andreessen and Blake Masters are linked to firms that co-invest with 1789 Capital in defense contractors, which have already secured billions in federal loans and contracts since the second Trump administration began. Critics argue these selections not only increase the influence of right-wing ideologues and autonomous weapons manufacturers but could also embed the financial interests of the president’s family directly into the setting of U.S. strategic defense priorities. Ethics experts warn of the risks of individuals advising on sectors where their financial interests are deeply vested.
