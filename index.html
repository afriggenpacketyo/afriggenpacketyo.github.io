Jockey Saffie Osborne has expressed that the "fear of failure" has dissipated following her historic victory in the St Leger, making her the first woman to win a British Classic. Osborne, who estimates driving 65,000 miles annually in a sport she describes as "very lonely," views this achievement as a lifelong dream fulfilled. She acknowledges the historical male dominance in racing but feels her gender is no longer a barrier to opportunities. Osborne aspires to become champion jockey, a feat no woman has yet accomplished. Her win is seen as a significant milestone, potentially opening more doors for female jockeys in the sport.

Alex Batty finds new purpose and job after six-year abduction ordeal

2026-09-17 01:14:53 EDT

Alex Batty, who was abducted by his mother and lived off-grid for six years, has found a renewed "sense of purpose" after securing employment at a waste management company. Batty, now 20, described the job as providing financial security for his young family and fulfilling his need to work, having started early in life. His mother, influenced by conspiracy theories, had instructed him to discard his passport, leading to his nomadic existence in France. Upon his return to his grandmother in Oldham, he faced a period of unemployment. Batty's story highlights the resilience of individuals overcoming extraordinary circumstances and the importance of stable employment in rebuilding one's life.
