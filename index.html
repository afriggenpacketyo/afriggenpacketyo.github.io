“Hang-xiety,” the pervasive anxiety that often accompanies a hangover, is a scientifically recognized phenomenon, according to psychopharmacology experts. Alcohol significantly impacts the brain's chemical messengers, GABA and glutamate, initially inducing relaxation but leading to increased alertness and arousal as it wears off, thereby triggering anxiety. A 2023 study of over 5,000 students revealed that 18.3% reported hangover-induced anxiety, with shy individuals being particularly susceptible; alcohol temporarily quiets social nerves, only for anxiety to rebound stronger the next day. Experts advise against using alcohol to manage stress and suggest reframing these feelings as a temporary physiological state rather than proof of social catastrophe. Recognizing hang-xiety as a transient condition can help mitigate its emotional impact and encourage healthier coping mechanisms.
