Redwood Trees Threatened by Extreme Heat's Impact on Photosynthesis

2026-10-06 06:00:00 EDT

A new study reveals that coast redwood trees are dialing down their photosynthesis during periods of extreme heat, a response that could have significant long-term consequences for these ancient giants and their ecosystems. When temperatures rise excessively, redwoods close their stomata to conserve water, which also halts their 'breathing' in of carbon dioxide, essential for growth. This 'carbon starvation' can weaken trees, making them less resilient and potentially impacting the diverse life that thrives in their canopies. Compounding the issue, the decline in coastal fog due to climate change further reduces their access to hydration and cooling, creating a double threat. While current recovery is possible, the long-term effects of increasingly frequent and intense heat waves remain a concern for the future of these iconic forests and the planet's climate.
