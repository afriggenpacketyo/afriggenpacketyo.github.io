The Trump administration has invoked executive authority to shut down 110 commercial driving schools, alleging connections to over 5,000 truck drivers who failed English language proficiency tests. Officials from the Departments of Transportation and Homeland Security singled out California as the primary problem state, linking drivers certified by these schools to 239 commercial motor vehicle-related deaths. The action includes a nationwide audit of third-party testers and a sweep of over 200 training schools. Critics argue the rules exacerbate a truck driver shortage and inflame anti-immigrant bias, while immigrant rights groups have challenged the license cancellations in court. This article highlights a contentious federal crackdown on the trucking industry.
