The former pardon attorney for the U.S. Justice Department, Elizabeth Oyer, has filed a lawsuit against the department and Attorney General Todd Blanche, alleging she was unlawfully fired after refusing to recommend restoring actor Mel Gibson's gun rights. Oyer claims she was asked to facilitate a "political favor" for Gibson due to his relationship with President Trump. She asserts her refusal to recommend the restoration of gun rights, which Gibson lost after a domestic violence conviction, led to her dismissal. The lawsuit seeks her reinstatement, back pay, and damages, arguing her firing violated her rights and principles of separation of powers. The Justice Department has previously denied that the Gibson case influenced her dismissal.

Tributes Pour In for Feminist Icon Gloria Steinem Following Her Death at 92

2026-09-03 15:04:19 EDT

Tributes are flooding in from across the political and cultural spectrum for feminist icon Gloria Steinem, who passed away at 92. Figures like Hillary Clinton, Jill Biden, and Lena Dunham have shared heartfelt remembrances, crediting Steinem with opening doors for women and advocating tirelessly for their rights. Steinem, a co-founder of Ms. magazine, was instrumental in bringing conversations about sexism and reproductive freedom into the mainstream. Her legacy is described as a "moral roadmap" for humanism and inclusion. Many vow to continue her fight for equality, acknowledging the challenges women's rights currently face.
