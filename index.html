2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

The Atlantic hurricane season is experiencing record quietness due to a strong El Niño phenomenon, which is suppressing storm development. As of mid-September, no hurricanes have formed, and only a few weak tropical storms have occurred, significantly below the average for this time of year. Experts attribute this to El Niño's influence, which creates high wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds that dismantle developing storms. While the current quiet is notable, forecasters caution against complacency, as a single storm can still cause significant damage.
