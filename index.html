Understanding Pneumonic Plague: Symptoms, Transmission, and Treatment

2026-10-06 11:35:22 EDT

Pneumonic plague is a severe lung infection, considered the most virulent form of plague, characterized by symptoms like shortness of breath, chest pain, and coughing up blood. While less common than bubonic plague, it is more serious and can spread from person to person through infected droplets, typically requiring close contact. The disease, caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, is endemic in rodent populations globally, with sporadic human cases occurring annually. Experts emphasize the importance of studying such diseases for diagnostics, vaccine development, and surveillance for antibiotic resistance. If left untreated, pneumonic plague is nearly always fatal, but it responds well to antibiotics if treatment begins early.
