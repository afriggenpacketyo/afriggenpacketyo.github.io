Kelvin Waves Raise West Coast Sea Levels, Increasing Flood Risk Amid 'Super El Niño'

The U.S. West Coast is currently experiencing elevated sea levels due to a Kelvin wave, a deep ocean phenomenon marking one of the first major signs of this year's "Super El Niño." These planetary-scale waves, first described by Lord Kelvin, develop along the equator from unusually warm Pacific waters and travel east, eventually splitting to move along the North and South American coasts. While not tsunamis, Kelvin waves gradually raise sea levels by six to eight inches, significantly increasing the risk of coastal flooding and erosion when combined with storms or high tides. Scientists predict several more Kelvin waves will impact the West Coast through fall and early winter, also bringing warmer water temperatures that can disrupt marine ecosystems.
