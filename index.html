Ranked Choice Voting System Explained Amidst Ongoing Debate and State Bans

2026-11-03 20:46:42 EST

Ranked choice voting (RCV), used in Maine and Alaska for congressional and state races, allows voters to rank candidates by preference. If no candidate secures a majority of first-choice votes, an instant runoff eliminates the lowest-ranked candidate, transferring their votes. Proponents argue RCV diminishes the "spoiler candidate" effect and encourages less negative campaigning, leading to winners with broader support. However, 19 states have banned the practice, with Alaska voters considering repeal, reflecting ongoing debates and misinformation surrounding its effectiveness and impact on political polarization.
