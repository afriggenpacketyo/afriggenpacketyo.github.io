Rhabdomyolysis: Understanding the Rare Condition Linked to Marathoner's Death

2026-11-03 14:21:32 EST

Rachel Apa, a 24-year-old participant in the New York City Marathon, tragically died from rhabdomyolysis, a rare and serious condition, her father confirmed. Rhabdomyolysis occurs when muscle cells break down, releasing myoglobin and other substances into the blood, which can severely damage the kidneys and cause deadly heart rhythms. Approximately 26,000 individuals in the United States are diagnosed with this condition annually, which can be triggered by very intense exercise, particularly in hot weather, or when dehydrated or sick. Common symptoms include muscle pain, weakness, and dark, tea-colored urine. Prompt medical attention and treatment with large amounts of IV fluids are crucial to mitigate the risk of kidney injury or failure, with doctors recommending a gradual increase in exercise intensity to prevent its onset.
