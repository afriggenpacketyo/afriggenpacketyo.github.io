Public health officials have issued a significant alert after an unvaccinated Amtrak passenger, confirmed to have measles, traveled extensively through six California counties before being hospitalized. The individual's route, spanning from Chicago to Los Angeles and then across various parts of California via both trains and buses, potentially exposed a large number of people. The California Department of Public Health has urgently advised residents to ensure their measles vaccination status is current, underscoring the disease's extreme contagiousness and its capacity for severe, lifelong, and even fatal consequences, particularly for children. Dr. Rita Nguyen stressed that the MMR vaccine is both safe and provides durable protection, recommending that exposed, unvaccinated individuals immediately contact healthcare providers and vigilantly monitor for symptoms over a 21-day period to prevent further community spread.

Opinion: Remembering Annie Dillard, Pulitzer-Winning Nature Writer and Philosopher

2026-09-19 08:00:00 EDT

This opinion article serves as a remembrance of Annie Dillard, the celebrated Pulitzer Prize-winning nature writer, who recently passed away at the age of 81. Dillard, best known for her seminal work "Pilgrim at Tinker Creek," dedicated her writing to exploring the profound mysteries of life, seamlessly blending meticulous observations of the natural world with deep philosophical inquiry. Describing herself as "spiritually promiscuous," she drew insights from a wide spectrum of religious and existential feelings, consistently seeking meaning amidst the bewildering beauty and inherent hazards of existence. The article highlights her distinctive, often blunt yet beautiful, prose, emphasizing her famous advice to writers: "Do not hoard what seems good... give it, give it all, give it now." Dillard's literary contributions profoundly opened avenues of understanding for countless readers, reflecting her enduring, loving bewilderment with the world.
