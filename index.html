The UN General Assembly has called for replacing the widely used Mercator map with a more accurate representation of continental sizes, officially adopting the Equal Earth projection. The Mercator map, designed in 1569 primarily for European sailors to navigate oceans, significantly distorts landmasses, making countries near the poles appear much larger and those near the equator, like Africa, seem considerably smaller than their true scale. Critics argue this historical distortion has psychological and political effects, implicitly diminishing the perceived importance of continents such as Africa. While the Equal Earth map successfully addresses these area discrepancies, it inherently sacrifices the straight lines crucial for navigation and cannot accurately measure distances, highlighting the fundamental impossibility of perfectly representing a three-dimensional globe on a two-dimensional chart.
