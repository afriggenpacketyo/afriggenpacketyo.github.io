Papua's "Eternal Snow" on Puncak Jaya Nears Complete Disappearance Due to Climate Change

2026-09-25 18:12:48 EDT

Papua's Puncak Jaya, historically known for its "eternal snow" near the equator, is facing the imminent and complete disappearance of its remaining glaciers within the next one to two years. Scientists report a dramatic reduction in glacier cover, shrinking from nearly 1,900 hectares in the 19th century to a mere 16 hectares today, representing the last vestiges of tropical ice between the Andes and Himalayas. This rapid glacial retreat is primarily driven by human-induced atmospheric warming and intensified by El Niño events, leading to the irreversible loss of a unique climate history archive preserved within the ice cores. For Indigenous communities like the Moni and Amungme, the mountain holds profound spiritual and cultural significance, symbolizing honesty and purity, rendering its impending disappearance a deep environmental and cultural tragedy.
