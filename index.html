The OCC's extensive review of nearly 100,000 complaints revealed that all nine banks used "reputation risk" policies lacking clear, objective standards, potentially disadvantaging customers based on their political or religious views or lawful business activities. This article highlights specific examples, such as JPMorgan subsidiary WePay closing accounts for merchants selling Trump merchandise, and Capital One declining relationships with both abortion-rights and gun-rights groups. The OCC is now considering new rules to prevent such discrimination, though it stopped short of announcing immediate fines or sanctions. (not investment advice)
