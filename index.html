This decision, based on the Trump administration's view that such practices "facilitate illicit drug use," has sparked significant concern among public health organizations like the Baltimore Harm Reduction Coalition. Critics argue that these test strips, which identify dangerous adulterants like fentanyl and benzodiazepines, are proven to change behavior and save lives. The article highlights fears that cutting this funding will lead to an increase in overdose deaths, forcing nonprofits to divert resources from other vital services.
