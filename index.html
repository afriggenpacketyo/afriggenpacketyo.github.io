Research Confirms the Reality of 'Hang-xiety' and Its Psychological Roots

2026-10-06 19:31:52 EDT

"Hang-xiety," the anxiety experienced during a hangover, is a recognized phenomenon stemming from alcohol's effects on brain chemistry, sleep, hormones, and the nervous system. Alcohol initially enhances GABA and suppresses glutamate, leading to relaxation, but as it wears off, the nervous system can rebound into heightened alertness and arousal, triggering anxiety. Studies indicate that while not everyone experiences it, and severity doesn't directly correlate with consumption, existing psychological traits like shyness can amplify its effects. Recognizing hang-xiety as a temporary physiological state rather than a social catastrophe can help mitigate its power.
