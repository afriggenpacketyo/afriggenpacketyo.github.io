The House has advanced the Ratepayer Protection Act, a bipartisan bill aimed at preventing artificial intelligence data centers from passing the costs of new infrastructure onto consumers. The legislation sets standards for large data centers (100 megawatts or more) to cover the expenses of new power sources and transmission lines. This move comes amid growing public backlash against data centers and their impact on utility bills. While the bill has broad support, some environmental groups argue it doesn't go far enough and could accelerate data center construction. The bill's passage is seen as a step towards addressing consumer concerns about rising energy costs associated with the burgeoning AI industry.

US Investigates Cyberattacks on Energy Tankers, Possible Iranian Link

2026-09-16 20:03:46 EDT

The U.S. Coast Guard and FBI are investigating cyberattacks that struck two Texas-bound energy tankers, the VL Prosperity and another unnamed vessel, last month. While U.S. officials have found evidence of malicious cyber activity, they have not publicly attributed the attacks to Iran, though Iranian state media claimed responsibility for the VL Prosperity incident. The attacks reportedly affected the ships' propulsion, navigation, and cargo systems. Modern vessels rely heavily on interconnected systems, making them vulnerable to cyber threats that could disrupt operations, cause pollution, or even lead to "remote hijacking." Experts warn that artificial intelligence could accelerate the sophistication of such attacks, emphasizing the need for robust cybersecurity measures in the maritime industry.

England Needs Seven Months of Heavy Rain to End Drought by Spring 2027

2026-09-16 20:01:29 EDT

New research indicates that England requires at least seven months of above-average rainfall to fully recover from drought by spring 2027. Despite recent wet weather, nearly three-quarters of England remains in drought following a dry spring and summer. The Environment Agency (EA) estimates that approximately 120% of typical rainfall is needed between now and April. Even with wetter-than-normal autumn forecasts, regions like Devon, Cornwall, and East Anglia could still face drought conditions by early 2027. Groundwater levels and reservoir storage remain low, emphasizing the ongoing seriousness of the situation and the need for continued water conservation efforts across the UK.
