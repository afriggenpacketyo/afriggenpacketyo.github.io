A rapidly strengthening El Niño is projected to bring the highest sea levels ever recorded to the California coast this year, significantly escalating the risk of widespread and damaging flooding and erosion. Forecasters warn this historically strong event could also unleash powerful rainfall and intense heatwaves across Southern California. Scientists explain that El Niño's warmer ocean water expands and displaces colder water, resulting in higher sea surface heights. This phenomenon, combined with potentially "enormously strong" waves, poses a severe threat to beaches and coastal infrastructure, with major flooding anticipated, particularly from late December through March. The potential for marine heatwaves also threatens kelp habitats and increases neurotoxin levels, impacting wildlife and fisheries.
