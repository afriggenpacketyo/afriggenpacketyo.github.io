Twenty-five years after the 9/11 attacks, families of the victims are hopeful that newly obtained records will provide compelling evidence of Saudi complicity, pressing the Trump administration to declassify more government documents. The families' lawsuit against the Saudi kingdom is nearing trial, and they believe recent findings, including a video purportedly casing the Capitol and a sketch of flight path calculations, constitute "smoking gun evidence." Brett Eagleson, whose father died in the attacks, urged President Trump to declassify these records, emphasizing the opportunity for resolution and justice. While the Saudi government denies involvement, the judge overseeing the case has previously found the evidence compelling enough to proceed to trial, suggesting a high probability of Saudi involvement through an alleged agent.

Trump Admin Proposes Ending Tax-Exempt Status for Private Colleges Using Race in Admissions

2026-09-03 17:00:21 EDT

The U.S. Treasury Department and IRS have issued proposed regulations that would end federal tax-exempt status for private schools using race in admissions or financial aid decisions, a move that could impact thousands of institutions. This proposal threatens a key tax break for donations to universities and could affect student scholarships. Experts suggest this is part of the administration's broader effort to dismantle diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) programs. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stated the rules aim to eliminate racial discrimination in the education system, warning that institutions using discriminatory practices will lose their tax benefits. The proposal does not affect schools with religion-based admissions. The potential loss of tax-exempt status could significantly reduce charitable donations to affected institutions.
