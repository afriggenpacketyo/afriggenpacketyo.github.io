The Federal Reserve is widely anticipated to raise its benchmark interest rate on Wednesday for the first time in over three years, a move aimed at combating persistent inflation fueled by high energy prices. Economists predict a 0.25 percentage point hike, with the possibility of additional increases in the coming months if inflationary pressures endure. This decision is expected to make borrowing more expensive for consumers, impacting credit cards and various loan products, although it could potentially stabilize longer-term interest rates such as those for mortgages. Conversely, savers can look forward to more attractive returns on high-yield savings accounts and certificates of deposit. Investors are advised to maintain well-diversified portfolios, as the financial markets have largely already factored in these anticipated monetary policy adjustments. (not investment advice)
