2026-11-24 03:34:00 EST

A nuclear explosion in space, while unlikely to cause direct injuries on Earth, could disable critical satellites essential for daily life, including those used for emergency communications and navigation. Historical tests like Starfish Prime in 1962 demonstrated that such blasts can generate electromagnetic pulses that disrupt electronics and create radiation belts, damaging satellites over time. While treaties prohibit nuclear weapons in space, detection is possible. Protecting against such an event could involve shielding and backups, but cascading debris from collisions, known as Kessler syndrome, remains a concern. Radioactive fallout reaching the ground from a space detonation is considered unlikely.
