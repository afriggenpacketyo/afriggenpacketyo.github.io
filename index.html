Research Confirms 'Hang-xiety' as a Real Phenomenon Linked to Brain Chemistry and Shyness

2026-10-06 19:31:52 EDT

"Hang-xiety," the pervasive anxiety accompanying a hangover, is a confirmed real phenomenon, explained by alcohol's intricate effects on brain chemicals GABA and glutamate. Alcohol initially enhances GABA, acting as the brain's braking system, and suppresses glutamate, which is involved in excitation, leading to feelings of relaxation and lowered inhibitions. However, as alcohol metabolizes, the brain's compensatory efforts to restore balance can trigger heightened alertness and anxiety. Research indicates that shy individuals are particularly susceptible, experiencing a stronger rebound of anxiety the day after drinking. Experts advise against using alcohol to manage stress and suggest reframing hang-xiety as a temporary state rather than a catastrophic social misstep.
