2026-10-14 21:30:12 EDT

A previously unknown dinosaur species, Invictibellatrix amberina, a "long-clawed predator the size of a tiger" and a cousin of the Tyrannosaurus rex, has been identified from a fossil overlooked for a century. The fossil, a skull fragment, was found on the Isle of Wight in 1924 and stored at the Natural History Museum in London. Researchers used CT scans to create a 3D model, revealing unique features that confirmed it as a new megaraptoran species. This 10 to 13-foot-long feathered carnivore, which lived around 120 million years ago, would have been a formidable hunter of smaller prey, potentially more dangerous to humans than a T. rex due to its size and hunting style.
