Ranked Choice Voting System Explained Amid Congressional Debates

2026-10-30 16:24:38 EDT

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is being used in congressional and state races in Maine and Alaska, prompting renewed debate over its effectiveness. Under RCV, voters rank candidates by preference; if no one gets a majority of first-place votes, the lowest-ranked candidate is eliminated, and their votes are reallocated based on second choices. Proponents argue RCV minimizes "spoiler" candidates and encourages more competitive, less negative campaigning, leading to winners representing a broader majority. However, critics cite its complexity and potential for confusion, with 19 states having banned the practice. Alaska voters will decide on a ballot question to repeal RCV this November.
