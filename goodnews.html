2026-11-18 21:02:23 EST

The government in England and Wales is poised to introduce new legislation aimed at abolishing the "feudal" leasehold system for flats, intending to replace it with a commonhold structure where residents collectively own and manage their buildings. Housing minister Matthew Pennycook criticized the current system as "blighting lives" and an "anachronism in the twenty-first century." The proposed Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill will also cap ground rent—the annual fee paid by leaseholders—at £250, progressively reducing it to a nominal "peppercorn" rate after 40 years. This significant reform seeks to bring England and Wales in line with shared ownership models prevalent globally, though freeholders express concerns about its impact on investment markets.
