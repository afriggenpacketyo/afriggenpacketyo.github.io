2026-11-24 17:34:00 EST

The European Space Agency's Euclid telescope has achieved a groundbreaking discovery, identifying J1504+6252, nicknamed Dún Aonghas, as the most distant and oldest quasar ever observed. This luminous object formed a mere 630 million years after the Big Bang, offering an unprecedented glimpse into the universe's infancy. A quasar represents the extremely bright core of an active galaxy, powered by gas falling into a supermassive black hole, and this particular one had already grown to 360 million times the mass of the Sun. This finding significantly supports theories that supermassive black holes developed remarkably quickly in the early universe, helping astronomers understand the formation and evolution of the first galaxies.
