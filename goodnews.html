The Scientific Transformation Behind Brussels Sprouts' Popularity Surge

2026-11-22 09:28:25 EST

Brussels sprouts, once a widely disliked vegetable, have become a culinary favorite thanks to a scientific breakthrough that eliminated their bitterness. Dutch biochemist Hans van Doorn, hired by Syngenta in 1988, identified and bred out two chemical compounds, sinigrin and progoitrin, responsible for the unpalatable taste. By 1993, he successfully developed sweeter varieties, leading to a nearly quintupled American consumption. This article highlights the impact of selective breeding and scientific innovation on food preferences, transforming a once-dreaded vegetable into a celebrated dish among chefs and the public.
