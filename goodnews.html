Study Reveals How Fatty Diets Increase Colorectal Cancer Risk Through Gut Bacteria

2026-08-17 02:49:00 EDT

German researchers have established a causal link between Western high-fat diets and an increased risk of colorectal cancer, particularly in adults under 50. The study shows that fatty foods sustain specific gut bacteria that produce deoxycholic acid (DCA), a secondary bile acid, which then promotes tumor growth by increasing cell division and DNA replication errors. Analyzing human stool samples confirmed higher DCA-producing bacteria in cancer patients. This foundational research not only explains the mechanism behind diet-induced cancer but also suggests potential future treatments like microbiome transplants and reinforces the importance of healthy eating and lifestyle choices for prevention.
