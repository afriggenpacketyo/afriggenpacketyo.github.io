New Sanctions Introduced for Criminals Refusing to Attend Sentencing in England and Wales

2026-10-22 03:17:39 EDT

Judges in England and Wales will gain stronger powers to impose sanctions on criminals who refuse to attend their sentencing, aiming to ensure they face victims' families. These new measures, effective next year, include solitary confinement for up to three weeks, social visit bans for nearly a month, and revocation of prison privileges for longer periods. The Ministry of Justice hopes these deterrents will rebalance the justice system towards victims, whose families have tirelessly advocated for such changes after experiencing the emotional impact of offenders skipping court. However, some critics question their effectiveness and potential impact on prisoners' relatives.
