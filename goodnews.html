Astronomers Discover Oldest and Most Distant Quasar, Featuring Massive Black Hole

2026-11-24 05:40:00 EST

Scientists have identified the most distant quasar ever found, named Dun Aonghasa, which also holds the record as the oldest, forming just 630 million years after the Big Bang. This discovery, made by a German-led team using the Euclid telescope and corroborated by Hubble and Webb, pushes back the cosmic timeline by nearly 40 million years. What makes this quasar particularly rare is the immense size of its central black hole, estimated at 360 million times the mass of our sun, despite being a relatively slow consumer of surrounding matter. This finding presents a significant puzzle for astrophysicists regarding the rapid growth of such massive black holes in the early universe.

Colombia's Unique Frailejon Plants Face Threats, Sparking Conservation Efforts

2026-11-24 05:00:00 EST

Colombia's high-mountain wetlands, known as páramos, are home to the unique frailejon plants, which are crucial for the country's water supply. These fuzzy, sunflower-related plants absorb water from cloud cover, maintaining moisture in the ecosystems that feed major rivers. However, frailejons face increasing threats from farming, ranching, coal mining, and climate change, with two-thirds of the nearly 150 species at risk. Conservationists like Moisés Moreno, a former coal miner, are leading reforestation efforts, while a popular cartoon character, Ernesto Pérez, is raising public awareness about the plant's importance. Volunteers are actively planting seedlings to protect these vital "water guardians."

Historic Black-Owned Gas Station on Route 66 Honored for Providing Refuge During Jim Crow Era

2026-11-24 05:00:00 EST

The Threatt Filling Station, located east of Oklahoma City, has been recognized on the National Register of Historic Places as the only known Black-owned and operated gas station along the historic Route 66. During the Jim Crow Era, when Black travelers faced widespread discrimination and exclusion from most services, the Threatt family offered fuel, food, and lodging. Founded in 1915 by Allen Threatt Sr., the station became a vital community hub and a safe haven between "sundown towns." Now, a century later, his grandchildren are actively restoring the derelict building, planning to expand it into an RV park and restaurant, continuing its legacy of welcome.
