The Neuroscience of Color Perception Explains Why Tennis Ball Color is Debated

2026-10-05 07:00:00 EDT

A neuroscientist explains that the debate over whether tennis balls are yellow or green highlights a fundamental aspect of color perception: color is not an inherent property of an object but a generative product of the brain. Bevil Conway, a neuroscientist at the National Eye Institute, clarifies that our brains actively construct color based on wavelength properties of light and contextual assumptions. This phenomenon, known as color constancy, allows us to perceive objects as having consistent colors despite varying lighting conditions. The specific hue of tennis balls, often fluorescent and positioned at the peak of human visual sensitivity, can be perceived differently depending on individual brain interpretations.
