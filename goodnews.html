Drinking Very Hot Beverages May Increase Risk of Esophageal Cancer, UK Study Suggests

2026-09-08 19:30:39 EDT

A large UK study suggests that regularly consuming beverages like tea and coffee at very high temperatures significantly increases the risk of esophageal cancer. Researchers found that individuals who drank beverages 'very hot' had approximately three times the risk compared to those who drank them warm. The study, involving nearly a million people, defines 'very hot' as 65 degrees Celsius or above, and consuming more than six hot drinks per day also raises the risk. While the overall chances of developing this cancer remain low, experts advise waiting for hot drinks to cool to a comfortable temperature before consumption to minimize potential damage to the food pipe's lining.
