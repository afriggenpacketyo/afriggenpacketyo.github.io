"Hang-xiety": The Real Science Behind Post-Drinking Anxiety

2026-10-06 19:31:52 EDT

"Hang-xiety," the pervasive anxiety often experienced during a hangover, is a scientifically recognized phenomenon stemming from alcohol's effects on the brain. Alcohol initially enhances GABA, the brain's calming system, while suppressing excitatory glutamate, leading to feelings of relaxation. However, as alcohol metabolizes, the brain attempts to restore balance, resulting in a rebound effect of heightened alertness and arousal that triggers anxiety. Research indicates that shy individuals are particularly vulnerable to this effect, experiencing significantly elevated anxiety markers. Experts recommend avoiding alcohol for stress management and reframing "hang-xiety" as a temporary physiological state rather than a social catastrophe.
