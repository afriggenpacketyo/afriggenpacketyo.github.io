NASA's Swift Observatory Expected to Reenter Earth's Atmosphere This Week

2026-11-02 14:00:07 EST

Most of the spacecraft is projected to burn up, but some dense tungsten and mirror parts could survive, with the risk of harm to anyone on Earth estimated as "unlikely," roughly 1 in 2,100. While wreckage will most likely fall into the ocean, parts of Africa, Australia, India, and the Americas are within the reentry zone. The Swift team mourns its loss but expresses gratitude for its 22 years of groundbreaking observations, which included discovering over 2,000 gamma-ray bursts and 1,400 exploding stars.
