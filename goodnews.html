Brighton's Royal Pavilion Showcases Indian Architectural Influence in England

2026-09-02 10:30:36 EDT

Brighton's Royal Pavilion stands as a striking example of Indian architectural influence in southern England, featuring Mughal arches and onion domes. Built in the late 18th century, it reflects British royals' fascination with South Asian design during their colonial rule in India. Queen Victoria once resided there, later decamping due to increasing public access. During World War I, it notably served as a military hospital, treating thousands of Indian soldiers fighting for the British, with separate kitchens catering to different castes. Now a museum, the pavilion's restored gardens host events, symbolizing a rich and complex cultural exchange across centuries.
