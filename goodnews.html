2026-08-22 13:40:52 EDT

Nancy Kassebaum Baker, the first woman elected to the U.S. Senate without succeeding a spouse, has died at 94. Serving three terms for Kansas from 1978, she was known for her moderate Republican politics, similar to her father, former Governor Alf Landon. Kassebaum Baker championed bipartisan efforts, notably co-authoring a health insurance law with Senator Ted Kennedy. Even after retiring, she remained active in public service and later broke with the Republican Party over its conservative shift, endorsing Democratic candidates and calling for President Trump's impeachment after the Capitol attack. Her legacy is defined by independence and dedication to public discourse.
