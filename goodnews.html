Scientists Identify Blood Biomarker for CTE Diagnosis in Living Patients

2026-11-13 23:00:33 EST

Scientists at Washington University School of Medicine have identified a blood biomarker, eMTBR-tau243, marking a significant advancement toward diagnosing chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) in living patients. This protein fragment is found to be elevated in advanced forms of CTE, a degenerative brain condition caused by repeated head trauma and common in contact sports. Currently, CTE can only be confirmed post-mortem through brain tissue examination. While the new test shows high accuracy in distinguishing advanced CTE from healthy individuals, it is not yet reliable for early-stage detection. If further large-scale studies validate these findings, this biomarker could revolutionize monitoring and enable trials for potential treatments for this debilitating disease.
