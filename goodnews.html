Physicist Francis Halzen Awarded Nobel Prize for Pioneering Neutrino Observatory

2026-10-06 06:16:28 EDT

Belgian physicist Professor Francis Halzen has been awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his instrumental role in establishing the IceCube Neutrino Observatory at the South Pole. This groundbreaking facility utilizes a cubic kilometer of Antarctic ice equipped with light sensors to detect high-energy neutrinos, often referred to as "ghost particles." These elusive subatomic particles serve as crucial astronomical messengers, carrying information from violent, high-energy processes occurring in distant galaxies. Halzen's vision and scientific leadership, dating back to 1988, have paved the way for a new kind of astronomy, enabling scientists to explore extreme cosmic conditions beyond the reach of conventional telescopes.
