Scientific Breeding Transforms Brussels Sprouts into a Popular Vegetable

2026-11-22 09:28:25 EST

Brussels sprouts, historically disfavored for their pronounced bitterness, have undergone a remarkable transformation into a popular culinary staple, largely due to decades of scientific selective breeding. Dutch biochemist Hans van Doorn, employed by agricultural seed company Syngenta, played a pivotal role by identifying sinigrin and progoitrin as the two chemical compounds responsible for the vegetable's unappealing taste. By 1993, van Doorn successfully bred these bitter elements out of the sprouts, yielding a significantly sweeter and more palatable variety. This scientific innovation, combined with the development of new recipes, has led to a nearly fivefold increase in American consumption, showcasing the powerful influence of agricultural research on evolving food preferences and market demand.
