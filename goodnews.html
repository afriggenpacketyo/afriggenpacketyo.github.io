Baby boomers, who are living longer, healthier, and more active lives, are increasingly drawing upon their substantial accumulated net worth from investments, retirement savings, and home equity to sustain robust spending patterns. This dynamic creates a stark contrast with younger generations, who are currently grappling with an "affordability crisis" and experiencing a decline in their median net worth. The article further indicates that investment withdrawals by older, higher-income individuals are now representing a growing share of consumer spending, underscoring a significant generational divide in financial stability and economic influence. Two American Economists Awarded Nobel Prize for Work on Managing Economic Shocks

2026-10-12 12:45:06 EDT

American economists Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford have been jointly awarded the Nobel memorial prize in economics for their groundbreaking work on models that significantly aid governments and central banks in managing various economic shocks. The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences recognized their contributions to the "microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilisation," which have provided a robust theoretical basis for economic policy utilized by institutions worldwide. Their research advanced beyond older Keynesian models by meticulously integrating how consumers and businesses make real-world decisions about investment and spending, thereby offering superior tools to navigate crises such as energy shocks or sudden shifts in household consumption. This prestigious award, formally known as the Bank of Sweden Prize in Economic Sciences, is valued at approximately $1.2 million and acknowledges their profound impact on modern economic management.
