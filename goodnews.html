Ancient Cave Art Suggests First Humans in Ireland Migrated from Wales

2026-09-05 01:52:05 EDT

The discovery of eight ancient cave art sites in southwest Ireland, dating back at least 15,000 years, suggests that the first humans in Ireland likely migrated from Wales. During the most recent Ice Age, a "lost land bridge" connected modern-day Pembrokeshire and southeast Ireland, offering an irresistible pathway for hunter-gatherers. Dr. George Nash, leading the Lost Land Bridge Project, posits that this forested and grassland plateau, rich in large herbivores, supported early human movement. Advances in artificial intelligence mapping aided this "exciting" discovery, pushing back the known timeline of human presence in Ireland by approximately 5,000 years and indicating potential for even earlier findings.
