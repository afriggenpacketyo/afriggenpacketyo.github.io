Ancient Cave Art Discovery Links Ireland's First Humans to Wales

2026-09-05 01:52:05 EDT

A groundbreaking discovery of ancient cave art in southwest Ireland, carbon-dated to at least 15,000 years ago, strongly suggests that the earliest human inhabitants of Ireland migrated from Wales. Researchers hypothesize that a "lost land bridge" emerged during the most recent Ice Age, connecting modern-day Pembrokeshire with the southeast tip of Ireland, offering an accessible route for hunter-gatherer communities. This archaeological finding significantly pushes back the timeline for human presence in Ireland by about 5,000 years, challenging previous assumptions. Utilizing advanced artificial intelligence, an international team located eight cave sites, and they believe the potential for uncovering even more ancient sites, particularly in bypassed coastal regions, remains exceptionally high, promising further insights into early European human movement.
