Old Debt Can Reappear on Credit Reports, But Strict Rules Govern Reporting Limits

2026-08-17 11:57:27 EDT

Amid rising credit card debt, borrowers may find old debts reappearing on their credit reports, but federal law sets strict limits on how long negative information can be reported. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), most negative credit data, including collection accounts and charge-offs, can remain for about seven years, tied to the original delinquency date. Debt collectors cannot 're-age' an account by changing this date, even if the debt is sold multiple times. It's crucial for consumers to verify the accuracy of such entries and dispute incorrect information, as making payments on very old debt can sometimes restart the statute of limitations for lawsuits, which differs from credit reporting periods. Debt Collectors Generally Cannot Garnish 401(k) Accounts Due to Federal Protections

2026-08-17 11:09:26 EDT

Despite rising household debt, most ordinary debt collectors and private creditors generally cannot garnish funds held in ERISA-qualified 401(k) accounts. The Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA) provides strong federal protections, preventing these benefits from being assigned or transferred to satisfy typical consumer debts. However, these protections are not absolute; exceptions exist for qualified domestic relations orders (e.g., child support, alimony) and federal tax debt. Crucially, these protections may not apply once funds are withdrawn from the 401(k) and deposited into a regular bank account, making early withdrawals to address debt a risky move with potential tax implications.
