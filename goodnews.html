New Research Explores Drug to Prevent Long-Term Effects of Childhood Trauma

2026-08-14 02:22:00 EDT

Researchers in Munich and Stockholm have published a study suggesting that the long-term social and physiological effects of early childhood adversity might be preventable with a novel drug. The study, conducted on mice, found that a compound called SAFit2, which blocks the protein FKBP51, could normalize stress responses and social behavior in mice exposed to early hardship. FKBP51 is crucial in regulating cortisol receptors, and its overexpression can keep the body's stress response activated. While promising, the drug was administered during the stressful period, indicating prevention rather than reversal, and its application to humans is still at least a decade away.
