Psychological technique of memory reconsolidation can boost self-esteem

2026-09-17 05:02:00 EDT

Psychologist Marisa Franco suggests that revisiting and reshaping difficult memories through a process called memory reconsolidation can significantly improve self-esteem. This technique, based on the work of Karim Nader, involves re-experiencing past events with an adult perspective, offering reassurance and catharsis to one's younger self. Franco explains that memories not only record the past but also predict the future, and by reframing them, individuals can reduce trauma symptoms and foster a stronger sense of self-worth. The "reparenting" exercise, where one's current self provides comfort to their younger self, is presented as a practical method to achieve these benefits, leading to greater self-acceptance and the ability to internalize positive experiences.

Mary Magdalene's image evolved from sinner to feminist icon through history

2026-09-17 03:06:00 EDT

The portrayal of Mary Magdalene has transformed significantly throughout history, evolving from a repentant prostitute to a feminist icon. Initially depicted as a penitent sinner, largely due to a 6th-century papal sermon that conflated her with other biblical women, her image began to shift with the Reformation and Renaissance. The rediscovery of the Gospel of Mary in modern times further bolstered her role as a close confidante of Jesus and an "Apostle of the Apostles." Today, she is recognized as a key figure in feminist theology and a role model for women, celebrated for living on her own terms. Recent artistic interpretations and the Catholic Church's elevation of her liturgical celebration underscore this evolving perception.
