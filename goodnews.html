Māori Kiwi Feather Cloak Returned to New Zealand from British Museum

2026-10-10 15:37:20 EDT

A significant cultural ceremony in Oxford marked the repatriation of a precious Māori kiwi feather cloak, known as a kahu kiwi, to New Zealand after more than a century in a British museum. The cloak was originally a farewell gift from Māori leaders in 1900 to Dr. George Cleghorn, a medical doctor who had worked in New Zealand, and was subsequently loaned to the Pitt Rivers Museum in 1913. The Homebound Project, a charity dedicated to funding such returns, facilitated its journey, describing the cloak as "crying to come home." This act represents a meaningful restoration of cultural heritage and a step towards reconciliation, highlighting the importance of indigenous artifacts returning to their ancestral lands.

Expedition Launched to Locate Amelia Earhart's Missing Plane Wreckage

2026-10-10 14:22:29 EDT

A team of researchers from the US-based Archaeological Legacy Institute (ALI) and Purdue University is en route to Nikumaroro Island in the Pacific Ocean, hoping to finally solve the mystery of famed aviator Amelia Earhart's disappearance. The expedition aims to investigate the "Taraia Object," an anomaly first spotted in satellite imagery in 2020 and confirmed in 1938 aerial photography, believed to be the wreckage of her Electra twin-engine plane. Earhart vanished in 1937 while attempting to circumnavigate the globe, leading to decades of speculation. This mission represents a renewed, tangible effort to find physical evidence and potentially conclude one of history's most enduring aviation mysteries.
