Powerful El Niño Contributes to Record-Low Atlantic Hurricane Season Activity

2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

The Atlantic hurricane season is experiencing record-low activity, with no hurricanes formed yet, largely due to a powerful El Niño phenomenon. This natural warming of the equatorial Pacific creates atmospheric conditions, including strong wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds, that effectively suppress storm formation in the Atlantic. While ocean temperatures are unusually warm, El Niño's atmospheric effects are currently dominant, hindering tropical cyclone development. Experts caution against complacency, however, noting that it only takes one storm to be catastrophic, as exemplified by Hurricane Andrew in 1992, despite the overall quiet season.
