Scientists Discover New Immune Hubs in Skull Bone Marrow, Potentially Aiding Brain Defense

2026-08-24 04:17:00 EDT

Researchers at Washington University School of Medicine have identified previously unknown immune "security stations" within the bone marrow of mouse skulls. These lymph node-like structures, which contain organized clusters of B and T cells, responded to brain tumors more rapidly than distant lymph nodes. Disrupting these hubs in mice accelerated tumor growth, while stimulating them improved tumor rejection. This discovery challenges the traditional view of the brain as "immune privileged" and suggests a dedicated local immune response, potentially opening new avenues for brain disease treatment.
