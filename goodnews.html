Scientists Discover Novel Immune Hubs in Skull Bone Marrow

2026-08-24 04:17:00 EDT

Researchers at Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis have identified previously unknown immune "security stations" within the bone marrow of mouse skulls. These lymph node-like structures were observed responding to brain tumors before more distant lymph nodes, suggesting a dedicated and potentially faster immune response for the brain. Disrupting these structures in mice accelerated tumor growth, while stimulating them improved tumor rejection. This discovery challenges the traditional view of the brain as "immune privileged" and opens new avenues for understanding and potentially treating brain diseases, although human evidence is still preliminary.
