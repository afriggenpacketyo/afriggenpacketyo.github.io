Byron Sher, a former Stanford law professor and California legislator who authored groundbreaking environmental laws, has died at 98. Sher was instrumental in creating legislation for recycling incentives, limiting inflated environmental claims, and combating water and air pollution. His 1988 legislation was the first to embed "global warming" into state law, forming the foundation for California's comprehensive climate change regime. Despite his quiet demeanor, Sher's ability to persuade and compromise led to significant environmental protections, leaving an indelible mark on California's landscape and policy, even impacting current infrastructure projects.
