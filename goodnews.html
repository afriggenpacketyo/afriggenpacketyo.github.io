Eastern Europe Commemorates Roma Holocaust Amidst Ongoing Antiziganism

2026-08-01 16:08:00 EDT

Eastern European countries commemorate the Roma Holocaust, or Porajmos, on August 2, honoring the 500,000 Roma and Sinti murdered by Nazis during World War II. This day, officially recognized by the European Parliament in 2015, marks the liquidation of the "Gypsy Family Camp" at Auschwitz-Birkenau. Nations like Poland, Czechia, and Slovakia hold ceremonies at memorial sites, including former concentration camps, with government participation, like Czech President Petr Pavel attending the Lety memorial opening. These commemorations also serve to raise awareness about ongoing Antiziganism and its contemporary consequences. Romania, Hungary, Bulgaria, Croatia, and North Macedonia also observe the day, though some without official recognition or with less public attention, reflecting diverse approaches to historical remembrance.
