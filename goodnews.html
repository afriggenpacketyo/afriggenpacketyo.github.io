Understanding attachment theory and developing secure relationships

2026-07-27 20:21:34 EDT

Attachment theory, developed by John Bowlby, posits that early caregiver bonds shape our relationship expectations. The theory outlines four styles: anxious, avoidant, fearful-avoidant, and secure. While often discussed in popular culture, experts caution against overgeneralization and the misconception that attachment styles are fixed. Research indicates that secure attachment is linked to better stress management and coping mechanisms. Change is possible, with secure attachment being the most stable. Understanding one's attachment style can foster self-awareness and lead to healthier relationships, though professional support may be needed for entrenched patterns.
