Understanding Ranked Choice Voting as Two States Utilize the System

2026-10-30 16:24:38 EDT

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is being utilized in congressional and state races in Maine and Alaska this year, allowing voters the opportunity to rank their preferred candidates. Under RCV rules, if no candidate secures over 50% of first-choice rankings, an instant runoff process eliminates the candidate with the fewest top rankings, reallocating their votes based on subsequent preferences until a majority winner emerges. Experts highlight RCV's main benefit as diminishing the role of "spoiler" candidates and encouraging more competitive, less negative campaigning among candidates seeking second and third choice endorsements. Despite its growing use, the system faces ongoing debate and opposition, with 19 states banning the practice, and Alaska voters set to decide on its repeal in an upcoming ballot question.
