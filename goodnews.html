Tax Implications of Covered Call ETFs Pose Hidden Challenges for Income-Seeking Investors

2026-10-19 08:58:03 EDT

Covered call ETFs, popular for their promise of monthly income, often conceal significant tax complexities for investors, particularly concerning non-dividend distributions, or return of capital. These distributions defer taxes by reducing an investment's cost basis, potentially leading to higher capital gains taxes upon sale, a surprise for many at tax time. Financial advisors strongly recommend holding these ETFs within tax-deferred accounts, such as traditional IRAs, to effectively mitigate immediate tax implications. The article also advises investors to thoroughly examine a fund's tax-cost ratio and Net Asset Value (NAV) growth, and to consider alternative income-generating investments like high-quality bonds, which may offer superior tax efficiency and lower expense ratios.
