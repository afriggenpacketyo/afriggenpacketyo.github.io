Tommy John, Pitcher Whose Pioneering Surgery Transformed Baseball, Dies at 83

2026-08-16 14:58:25 EDT

Tommy John, the legendary MLB pitcher whose career-saving elbow surgery became known by his name, has died at 83. In 1974, facing a career-ending torn ulnar collateral ligament, John underwent an experimental procedure by Dr. Frank Jobe, replacing the ligament with a tendon. This "Tommy John surgery" has since saved thousands of pitchers' careers, including major stars like Shohei Ohtani. John pitched 14 more seasons after the operation, winning 164 games post-surgery, far surpassing his pre-injury record. Though he never made the Hall of Fame, his impact as an athletic and medical pioneer is immeasurable, despite his later concerns about the surgery's prevalence in young athletes.
