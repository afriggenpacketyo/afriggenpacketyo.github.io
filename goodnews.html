Ancient Mammal Fossil Reveals Earliest Evidence of Chewing and Suckling

2026-10-07 15:59:16 EDT

A remarkably well-preserved 165-million-year-old mammal fossil, named Megacauda sungei and unearthed in Inner Mongolia, offers the earliest evidence of both chewing and suckling abilities in ancient mammalian relatives. Paleontologists Zhe-Xi Luo and Thomas Martin describe this chihuahua-sized creature, which uniquely combined a platypus-like tail, suggesting semi-aquatic foraging, with predatory teeth capable of shearing food. Advanced X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy and CT scans revealed intricate hyoid bones and mouth structures, enabling scientists to reconstruct its sophisticated feeding mechanisms. This significant discovery pushes back the evolutionary timeline for these fundamental mammalian traits, underscoring the early diversification and adaptive success of mammals during the dinosaur era.
