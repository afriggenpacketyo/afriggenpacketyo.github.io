UK Court Overturns Anti-Gay Laws in Trinidad and Tobago, Landmark Ruling for LGBTQ+ Rights

2026-11-10 13:04:18 EST

London's Judicial Committee of the Privy Council (JCPC) overturned laws criminalizing same-sex intimacy in Trinidad and Tobago, a landmark decision that could impact other Commonwealth nations. The ruling, stemming from an 11-year legal battle initiated by dual-citizen Jason Jones, found the colonial-era laws violated the "fundamental individual right to respect for private and family life." This is the first time the JCPC, the Caribbean island's final court of appeal, has decided on a case to decriminalize same-sex intimacy, offering significant hope for campaigners seeking full equality across the Commonwealth. The judgment affirms that such laws "cannot be regarded as reasonably justifiable in a society that has a proper respect for the rights and freedoms of an individual."
