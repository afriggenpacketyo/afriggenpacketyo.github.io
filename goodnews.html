2026-10-19 01:28:02 EDT

Nearly 60 years after the Aberfan disaster, a new BBC program highlights how portraits of the 116 child victims brought comfort to grieving families. Artist William Richardson, deeply moved by the 1966 tragedy where a coal tip collapsed on a school, painted around 100 portraits from photographs and parents' descriptions. These artworks became cherished representations, helping families keep the memory of their lost children alive. The article shares poignant stories of families' experiences with these portraits, underscoring their profound role in the healing process and preserving the children's personalities.

Cricket Rule Change Revitalizes Reverse Swing in One-Day Internationals

2026-10-19 01:27:56 EDT

A rule change implemented in July 2025, allowing captains to select one of two white balls for the final 16 overs of an ODI innings, has led to a 'reverse swing renaissance' in cricket. This modification was intended to assist bowlers, and data indicates a significant increase in the amount of swing found by pace bowlers in the death overs. Consequently, pacers' economy rates and bowling averages have improved, making it harder for batsmen to score freely at the end of an innings. England has notably benefited from this change, with tactical adjustments expected across teams ahead of the next World Cup.

Government Proposes £100 Fines for Loud Music on English Buses

2026-10-18 20:22:20 EDT

These initiatives also target littering, vaping, and threatening conduct, seeking to improve overall journey experiences. Local authorities will gain new powers to impose these fixed penalties, though bus drivers will not be responsible for enforcement; authorized officers and police will handle incidents. A national campaign, 'share the carriage, not the noise,' is also launching for rail passengers, aiming for consistent messaging across the network to foster a more respectful travel environment.
