Scientists Link Specific Sleep Habits to a Newly Proposed Subtype of Depression

2026-11-07 15:28:59 EST

Scientists propose a new subtype of depression, termed "circadian depression," characterized by specific sleep habits linked to the body's internal clock. A study of over 11,000 Australian adults identified about 23% meeting criteria, including being an evening person, chronic fatigue upon waking, increased sleep during depressive periods, seasonal changes, late sleep midpoint, and "social jetlag." Individuals in this circadian group reported earlier onset, more severe symptoms, and poorer responses to common antidepressants like SSRIs and SNRIs. This research suggests that sleep-wake disruption might be an underlying cause rather than merely a symptom, potentially guiding more personalized treatment approaches in the future.
