2026-10-23 07:09:08 EDT

Chinese scientists have achieved a significant breakthrough by successfully reconstructing the facial features of Denisovans, a mysterious species of ancient humans previously known primarily through genetic evidence and fragmented fossil remains. This pioneering work is founded on fossils excavated from the Xujiayao archaeological site in northern China's Nihewan Basin, discovered between 1976 and 1979. Researchers meticulously confirmed that the collection of ancient human remains belonged to the Denisovan lineage, which then allowed them to create a detailed visual representation of what these hominids, believed to have inhabited parts of Siberia and East and Southeast Asia, might have looked like thousands of years ago. This advancement offers new and invaluable insights into human evolutionary history.
