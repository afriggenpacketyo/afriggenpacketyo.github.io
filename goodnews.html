Chinese Team Unearths World's Oldest Amber in Xinjiang

2026-07-27 21:00:07 EDT

A team of Chinese palaeontologists, under the leadership of Luo Cihang from the Chinese Academy of Sciences’ Nanjing Institute of Geology and Palaeontology, has made a groundbreaking discovery: the world's oldest known amber. Hundreds of microscopic pieces of this ancient resin were found remarkably preserved within a coal seam in Xinjiang, with dating placing them in the Middle Devonian period, approximately 393 million to 383 million years ago. This significant find pushes back the timeline for confirmed amber by tens of millions of years, surpassing the previously known oldest samples from the Late Carboniferous period. The researchers initially approached the discovery with skepticism, publishing their detailed findings in the peer-reviewed journal Science Advances.

While popular on social media for self-assessment, experts like Dr. Pascal Vrticka caution against overgeneralization and the misconception that attachment styles are fixed.
