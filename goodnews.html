Nobel Prize in Medicine Awarded for Optogenetics Research Advancing Brain Understanding

2026-10-05 07:04:37 EDT

The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine has been awarded to US psychiatrist and neurologist Karl Deisseroth and German colleagues Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for their pioneering work in optogenetics. Their research has revolutionized neuroscience, providing a method to control cell electrical activity using light and illuminate brain pathways. This breakthrough has enabled scientists to map neural circuits behind specific memories, feelings, and behaviors relevant to neurological and psychiatric disorders. Optogenetics is now being explored in clinical medicine, including efforts to restore sight in visually impaired individuals, fundamentally altering our understanding of the brain's complex workings.

Neuroscientist Explains Why People Disagree on Tennis Ball Color

2026-10-05 07:00:00 EDT

A neuroscientist explains that the common debate over whether tennis balls are yellow or green stems from the fact that "there's no such thing as a color" existing independently in the world. Color is a generative product of the brain, dependent on an individual's visual system interacting with light and making assumptions about lighting conditions. This phenomenon, known as color constancy, allows our brains to perceive an object's color as consistent despite varying light. The article highlights how this brain-created reality explains viral phenomena like "the dress" and why people can become unnerved when others perceive colors differently. Tennis balls, being fluorescent and optimally detected by our visual system, fall into a range where perception can lean towards either yellow or green.
