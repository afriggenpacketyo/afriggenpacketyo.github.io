While 95% of brain cells are shared, the remaining 5% drive distinct behaviors such as male aggression, courtship, and "love songs," with unique wiring identified for visual tracking and aggression. Researchers emphasize this breakthrough, likened to a new telescope for neuroscience, will not explain human gender differences but could profoundly advance understanding of how genes influence brain wiring and offer clues for complex human conditions like autism and schizophrenia.
