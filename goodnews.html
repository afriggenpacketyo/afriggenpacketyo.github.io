Research Clarifies Misconceptions About Crime Rates Among Non-German Nationals

2026-10-14 04:48:00 EDT

A new research paper led by criminologist Susann Prätor in Germany critically examines the common perception that non-German nationals commit more crimes than German citizens. The study reveals that while police crime statistics indeed show higher rates for non-Germans, this data is significantly skewed by a reporting bias, as foreign nationals are twice as likely to be reported to the police. After adjusting for this bias, the actual disparity in crime rates between the two groups is substantially reduced. The research identifies demographic factors like being young, male, poorly educated, and having a history of violence as key predictors of criminal behavior, rather than cultural background.
