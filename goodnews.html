2026-11-03 20:46:42 EST

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is being utilized in congressional and state races in Maine and Alaska, allowing voters to rank candidates by preference rather than choosing a single one. This system ensures a majority winner by reallocating votes from eliminated candidates with the fewest first-place rankings until one secures over 50%. Proponents argue RCV diminishes the "spoiler effect" of third-party candidates and incentivizes less negative campaigning, as candidates seek second and third-choice endorsements to gain broader support. Despite its use, RCV faces significant opposition, with 19 states banning the practice, and Alaska voters are currently considering its repeal. The article emphasizes the importance of understanding RCV's mechanics amidst growing political polarization and misinformation.

Understanding the Timeline for Determining US Midterm Election Winners
