Electric Cars May Be Causing Motion Sickness in Some Passengers

2026-10-02 22:36:19 EDT

Some passengers are reporting increased motion sickness in electric vehicles (EVs), a phenomenon attributed to sensory mismatches. Experts suggest the absence of traditional engine sounds and vibrations in EVs can disrupt the body's perception of movement, while regenerative braking, if not smoothly executed, can cause sudden decelerations that induce nausea. Studies indicate some individuals experience more severe motion sickness in EVs compared to conventional cars, despite many EV drivers reporting no issues. As EV adoption grows, with new petrol and diesel car sales banned from 2030 in the UK, passengers are advised to look out the front window, keep their heads still, and recline seats to alleviate symptoms, while carmakers explore solutions like smoother acceleration software and haptic feedback.
