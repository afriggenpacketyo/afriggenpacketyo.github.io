Understanding and Managing Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD)

2026-10-31 05:01:00 EDT

Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD), affecting about 1 in 20 Americans, is a condition where individuals experience depression or sluggishness during colder, darker months, often reversing in spring. Psychiatrist Dr. Norman Rosenthal, who coined the term, notes symptoms like oversleeping, overeating, and disinterest in hobbies. The article provides five key strategies for managing SAD: recognizing symptoms, utilizing light therapy (SAD lamps), maintaining thoughtful and consistent eating habits, incorporating regular exercise, and reducing stress through activities like meditation or therapy. These proactive measures can significantly alleviate symptoms, though medication remains an option for severe cases.
