Experts Explain Jet Lag and Offer Strategies for Adjustment

2026-08-05 19:49:46 EDT

Jet lag, a common consequence of rapid travel across time zones, occurs when the body's internal clock, or circadian rhythm, struggles to align with the new local time. Dr. Nina Rzechorzek of Cambridge University explains that this mismatch causes disturbed sleep, daytime sleepiness, and other issues as the brain's master clock adjusts to new light-dark cycles. Dr. Anna Edmondson adds that while almost all body cells have their own 24-hour clocks, the master clock, set by environmental cues like sunlight, takes several days to reset. Understanding these biological mechanisms is key to mitigating jet lag's effects.
