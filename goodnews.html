A new art exhibit and an article in The Atlantic highlight the profound, yet often overlooked, influence of Milton Avery on Mark Rothko and Adolph Gottlieb. The three artists spent summers together on Cape Ann, Massachusetts, in the 1930s, where Avery, as a mentor, encouraged their artistic development through example rather than direct instruction. While Rothko and Gottlieb later broke away to pursue abstraction, Avery's unique approach to color and composition, influenced by Bonnard and Matisse, laid a foundational groundwork. The article argues that Avery's oeuvre, with its connection to the material world, ultimately proves more richly poetic and generative than the later, more formulaic works of his protégés.
