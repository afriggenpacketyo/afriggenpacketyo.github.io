NASA Orbiter Discovers Massive New Moon Crater, Largest in Recent Times

2026-09-16 17:45:43 EDT

NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) has discovered a new moon crater, larger than the Roman Colosseum, formed by a powerful impact two years ago that went initially undetected. Measuring 728 feet across and up to 141 feet deep, this is the solar system's biggest known impact crater carved out in recent times. Such a large-scale event is estimated to occur only once every 132 years, making this a treasure trove of information as NASA prepares to build a base for astronauts on the moon. The impact churned the lunar surface for over 66 miles, and a separate study identified a 4-mile-wide cold spot around the crater, consistent with a loosening of the moon's top soil.

2026-09-16 16:14:55 EDT

New academic research suggests that artificial intelligence, particularly large language models, could inadvertently make people "dull" by promoting predictable and normative information. Columbia Business School professor Sandra Matz's study found that AI, by predicting the most likely next word or event, homogenizes decisions and steers users towards common choices, reducing distinctive behaviors and preferences. Matz argues that AI "hates risk" and is programmed to keep users on platforms by showing them what they already like, thereby narrowing exploration across topics and psychological affinities. To counteract this potential diminishing of human experience, she encourages developers to integrate an "exploration mode" to foster more unexpected and diverse recommendations.
