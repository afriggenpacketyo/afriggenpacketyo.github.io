Euclid Telescope Discovers Most Distant Quasar, Offering Insights into Early Universe

2026-11-24 17:34:00 EST

The European Space Agency's Euclid telescope has led to the discovery of Dún Aonghas, the most distant known quasar, formed just 630 million years after the Big Bang. This ancient object, observed as the universe was only 5% of its current age, offers valuable information about the early cosmos. Quasars, powered by gas falling into supermassive black holes, represent a brief, extremely bright phase in a galaxy's life. This discovery supports the theory that supermassive black holes formed remarkably quickly through the direct collapse of large gas clouds and rapidly accumulated matter, outshining their host galaxies. The light from Dún Aonghas carries crucial data about how the first galaxies evolved.
