El Niño May Lead to Unprecedented Lack of Atlantic Hurricanes This Season

2026-10-02 19:37:13 EDT

For the first time in over 30 years, the Atlantic basin has not seen a single hurricane form throughout September, which is traditionally the most active month for tropical cyclone activity. This unprecedented quietude significantly raises the possibility that the 2026 Atlantic hurricane season could be the only one in the satellite era, dating back to the 1960s, without a single hurricane development. The primary meteorological driver behind this phenomenon is a very strong El Niño, a climate pattern known to traditionally stifle activity in the Atlantic while simultaneously bringing a very active season to the eastern North Pacific. Forecasters had indeed predicted a quieter-than-normal season, and with the typical peak now passed, a hurricane-free season appears increasingly likely, marking a stark departure from historical patterns.
