Byron Sher, a former Stanford law professor and California legislator renowned for authoring some of the state's most far-reaching environmental laws, has died at 98. Sher, known for his cerebral and soft-spoken demeanor, championed legislation promoting recycling, combating water and air pollution, and preserving ancient redwoods, notably Headwaters Forest. His 1988 bill, which first embedded "global warming" into state law, laid the foundation for future climate change efforts. Despite his quiet approach, Sher left an indelible mark, demonstrating an ability to persuade across partisan lines and shaping California's environmental landscape for decades. He was also instrumental in creating state wild and scenic rivers.
