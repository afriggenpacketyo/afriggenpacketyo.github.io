Germany Considers Significant Reforms to its Minijob Employment System

2026-08-01 04:49:00 EDT

Germany is actively debating the future of its "minijob" employment system, which currently allows individuals to earn up to €603 monthly without social insurance or income tax contributions, making it popular among students and women. Critics contend that these jobs reinforce gender inequality and precarious working conditions, hindering long-term financial security and pension building. Proposed government reforms aim to raise the flat tax for employers and eliminate the pension contribution opt-out, effectively integrating minijobbers into regular social security systems. This initiative seeks to bolster social security, mitigate old-age poverty risks, and address existing gender disparities, despite concerns from businesses about losing flexibility and from students about potential income reductions.
