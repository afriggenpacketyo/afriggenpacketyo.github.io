Ranked Choice Voting Explained as Two States Utilize System in Congressional Races

2026-11-03 20:46:42 EST

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is being utilized in congressional and state races in Maine and Alaska, allowing voters to rank candidates by preference rather than selecting a single choice. This system ensures a majority winner by reallocating votes from the lowest-ranked candidates until one achieves over 50% support. Advocates contend that RCV minimizes the "spoiler effect" of third-party candidates and encourages more positive, less adversarial campaigning, ultimately leading to more representative electoral outcomes. However, the system faces significant opposition, with 19 states banning the practice, and Alaska voters are currently deciding on a ballot question that could repeal its use, underscoring ongoing debates about its complexity and perceived fairness.
