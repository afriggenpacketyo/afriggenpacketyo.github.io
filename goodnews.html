Understanding the Necessity of a Power of Attorney Alongside a Will for Estate Planning

2026-11-24 14:02:00 EST

This article clarifies that while a will dictates asset distribution after death, a power of attorney (POA) is crucial for managing finances during one's lifetime, especially in cases of incapacitation. A will's executor typically gains authority only post-mortem, leaving a gap for immediate financial needs like bill payments. A durable financial POA allows a designated agent to handle various financial matters, preventing potential court authorization delays and expenses for families. Therefore, having both a will and a POA ensures comprehensive protection and clear guidance for loved ones, addressing different phases of financial management.
