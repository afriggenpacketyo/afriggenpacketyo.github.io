Liechtenstein Amends Royal Succession Laws to Include Women

2026-08-15 15:41:57 EDT

Liechtenstein, a small central European country of 44,000, has announced a historic change to its royal succession laws, now allowing women to ascend the throne. This declaration, made on the national day, transitions the order from male primogeniture to absolute primogeniture, meaning the first-born child, regardless of sex, will become heir. While the current heir, Hereditary Prince Alois, remains unaffected, the new rule will apply to the descendants of his four children. This reform ensures that both the Principality and the Princely House will be led by the person best prepared for the responsibility, aligning Liechtenstein with modern succession practices seen in other European monarchies like Britain.
