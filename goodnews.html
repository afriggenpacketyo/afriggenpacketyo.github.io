Understanding and Managing Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD)

2026-10-31 05:01:00 EDT

Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD), a condition causing depression and lethargy during darker months, affects approximately 1 in 20 people in the U.S. Symptoms typically include sluggishness, oversleeping, and loss of interest in activities. The article outlines strategies for managing SAD, emphasizing the importance of light therapy using SAD lamps to regulate circadian rhythms. Consistent eating habits, stopping meals three hours before sleep, and regular physical activity are also crucial for signaling the body's internal clocks. Reducing stress through various methods like meditation and making pleasurable plans can further alleviate symptoms, though medication may be necessary in severe cases. Early recognition and proactive management are key to mitigating SAD's impact.
