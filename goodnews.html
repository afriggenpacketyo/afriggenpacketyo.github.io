Very Hot Drinks Linked to Increased Oesophageal Cancer Risk

2026-09-08 19:30:39 EDT

New UK research suggests that regularly consuming "very hot" beverages, such as tea and coffee, significantly increases the risk of oesophageal cancer, also known as food pipe cancer. This extensive study, encompassing nearly a million people, found that individuals who consumed drinks at 65 degrees Celsius or above faced approximately three times higher risk compared to those who drank "warm" beverages. Experts attribute this to the potential for steaming hot liquids to damage the cell lining of the food pipe. The recommendation is to allow hot drinks to cool for a few minutes before consumption, ensuring they reach a comfortable and safer temperature.
