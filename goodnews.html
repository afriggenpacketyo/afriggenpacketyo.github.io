Newly Discovered "Coffee Frogs" in Costa Rica Face Threat from Pesticides

2026-08-08 15:48:58 EDT

A tiny, nocturnal greenish frog, dubbed the "coffee frog," has been identified as a new species endemic to Costa Rica, thriving in the country's central Pacific coffee plantations. First observed in the 1960s but misidentified, its unique genetic and morphological traits were confirmed in 2021. Males are distinguished by a translucent throat that inflates with their high-pitched calls heard at night. However, this newly recognized species faces significant threats from agrochemicals used in coffee farming, as pesticides can seep into aquatic systems where tadpoles develop, alongside other dangers like waste dumping and habitat loss due to increased tourism.
