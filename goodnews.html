Understanding and Managing Evening Snacking Habits

2026-11-05 19:42:05 EST

Research indicates that about a third of people snack nightly after dinner, with four in five young adults unable to resist, a habit linked to weight gain and obesity, especially when eating within two hours of bedtime. Scientists explain that the "hunger hormone" ghrelin naturally peaks later in the day, and our appetite for calorie-dense foods strengthens, driven by the brain's reward system rather than a genuine need for energy. Additionally, not having a nutritious or satiating evening meal can lead to a "crash" later. To manage this common behavior, experts advise identifying the root cause—genuine hunger versus habit—and suggest making earlier meals more filling, changing routines, and planning healthy snacks in advance. Moderation and making healthy options the default are key to success.
