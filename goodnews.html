Tracing the Chinese Origins of the Phrase 'Gung-Ho' Amidst US-China Diplomacy

2026-09-25 06:38:11 EDT

US President Donald Trump's reference to the "Chinese phrase" "gung-ho" during his welcome of Chinese President Xi Jinping at the White House sparked linguistic curiosity, particularly among Chinese audiences. The term "gung-ho" originates from the Chinese "gong he" (工合), an abbreviation for "gongye hezuoshe" (工业合作社), meaning "industrial cooperatives." These were vital grassroots labor units established in China in 1938, dedicated to producing industrial supplies for the war effort against Japan. This shared historical linguistic root, though perhaps unfamiliar to many, highlighted a subtle connection between the two nations' pasts. The article provides an interesting cultural footnote to the diplomatic exchanges between the two global powers.

Agrivoltaics: Integrating Solar Panels with Agriculture to Boost Crop Yields and Energy Production

2026-09-25 05:31:00 EDT

Agrivoltaics, the practice of co-locating solar panels with agriculture, is emerging as a promising strategy to boost crop yields and renewable energy production. This innovative approach involves growing crops or grazing livestock beneath solar panels, which provide shade, divert water, and create cooler, damper microclimates that can enhance plant growth. The panels themselves also operate more efficiently when kept cool by transpiring plants. While offering a "double climate win" by cutting emissions and aiding adaptation to warming, agrivoltaics currently represents less than 1% of global solar capacity due to challenges like cost, varying effectiveness in different climates, and farmer skepticism.
