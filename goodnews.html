Newly Discovered Costa Rican "Coffee Frogs" Face Pesticide Threat

2026-08-08 15:48:58 EDT

A tiny, nocturnal greenish frog, affectionately dubbed the "coffee frog," has been identified as a new species endemic to Costa Rica's central Pacific region, thriving within coffee plantations. Measuring less than four centimeters, these amphibians are notable for the males' distinctive high-pitched calls, which emanate from translucent throats as they perch on coffee shrubs at night. While frogs naturally seek shade, moisture, and insects within these agricultural habitats, researcher Wagner Chaves-Acuna warns that pesticide use poses a significant threat. These agrochemicals can travel through the air and seep into the soil, contaminating the aquatic systems essential for tadpole development and jeopardizing the species' long-term survival.
