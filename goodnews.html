Regularly Consuming Very Hot Drinks Linked to Increased Oesophageal Cancer Risk

2026-09-08 19:30:39 EDT

New UK research, the largest of its kind to date, suggests that regularly drinking beverages at very hot temperatures significantly increases the risk of oesophageal cancer, also known as cancer of the food pipe. The study, involving nearly a million people, found that those consuming "very hot" drinks, defined as 65 degrees Celsius or above, had approximately three times higher risk compared to "warm" drinkers. Steaming hot liquids can damage the cell lining of the food pipe as they pass through, and consuming six or more hot drinks daily further elevates this risk. Experts advise waiting a few minutes for drinks to cool to a comfortable temperature before consumption to mitigate potential health hazards.
