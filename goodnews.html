Germany Considers Eliminating 'Minijobs' to Strengthen Pension System and Address Gender Inequality

2026-08-01 04:49:00 EDT

Germany is contemplating a significant reform of its 'minijob' system, which allows individuals to earn up to €603 per month without social insurance or income tax contributions. While these jobs are popular among students and women for their flexibility, critics argue they perpetuate gender inequality and prevent adequate pension accumulation. The proposed changes include eliminating the opt-out for pension contributions and making minijobbers subject to regular social security contributions, effectively ending the current form of minijobs. Supporters believe this will strengthen social security, reduce old-age poverty, and improve career development for women, while some business representatives express concern about flexibility and labor potential.

Clubs like Tottenham have seen substantial increases in matchday and commercial revenue due to their modern stadiums, which can host non-football events. Crucially, stadium infrastructure costs are excluded from SCR calculations, allowing clubs to invest more in players without violating spending rules.
