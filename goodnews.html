Ranked Choice Voting Process Explained Amidst Congressional Races

2026-11-03 20:46:42 EST

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is being utilized in congressional and state races in Maine and Alaska, allowing voters to rank candidates by preference. Under RCV, if no candidate secures over 50% of first-choice votes, the candidate with the fewest top rankings is eliminated, and their votes are reallocated based on voters' subsequent preferences until a majority winner emerges. The article explains that RCV aims to minimize the impact of "spoiler" candidates and can encourage more competitive and less negative campaigning. Despite its benefits, RCV faces opposition, with 19 states banning the practice and Alaska voters considering a repeal, amidst political polarization and misinformation about the system's mechanics.
