2026-10-22 21:00:06 EDT

Chinese researchers, leveraging the world's largest single-dish radio telescope, FAST, have made an "extremely rare" discovery: a stable three-star system situated about 7,000 light-years from Earth. This celestial arrangement contrasts sharply with the chaotic multi-sun systems often imagined in science fiction, such as Liu Cixin's "The Three-Body Problem." The observed system comprises two dead stars—a pulsar rotating over 300 times per second and a white dwarf—locked in an eight-day orbit around each other, accompanied by a third "living" star in a remarkably steady cosmic dance. This significant astronomical finding provides valuable real-world data on complex stellar dynamics and the long-term stability of multi-star configurations, expanding our understanding of the universe.
