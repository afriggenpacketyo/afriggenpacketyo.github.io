This article explains that the El Niño phenomenon generates strong wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds, effectively "decapitating" and "starving" potential storms. While Atlantic waters are warm, the atmosphere above is heating faster, reducing the critical temperature difference needed to fuel hurricanes. Experts warn against complacency, citing past catastrophic storms during El Niño years, and predict next year's season could be extra busy as these warm waters persist.
