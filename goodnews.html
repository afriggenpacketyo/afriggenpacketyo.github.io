Kagan and Soai Awarded Nobel Prize in Chemistry for Solving Life's Asymmetry

2026-10-07 07:51:52 EDT

French scientist Henri B. Kagan and Japan's Kenso Soai have been awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for their groundbreaking work on life's asymmetry, specifically how nature exclusively produces one mirror-image version of molecules. Their discoveries addressed the mystery of "chirality," which is crucial for drug development, as different molecular mirror images can have vastly different biological effects. Kagan first manipulated chemical reactions to favor one image in 1986, while Soai later achieved homochirality in 2003, controlling reactions to produce only the desired molecular version. This is a powerful example of fundamental chemistry underpinning societal solutions.
