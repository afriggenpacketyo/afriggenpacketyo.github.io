Understanding "High-Functioning Depression" and Anhedonia: The Silent Loss of Joy

2026-10-29 16:00:00 EDT

Psychiatrist Dr. Judith Joseph highlights "high-functioning depression," a condition where individuals maintain daily obligations while quietly losing the ability to feel pleasure, known as anhedonia. Joseph describes anhedonia as the "silent sister" of depression, often overlooked compared to more overt symptoms. Her research, published in Cureus, links high-functioning depression to increased levels of anhedonia and trauma, particularly in parents and caregivers. Since it's not an official diagnosis, many may not seek help until burnout. Joseph offers a self-assessment quiz and a "Five V's" framework—Validation, Venting, Values, Vision, Vitals—to help individuals reclaim their joy.
