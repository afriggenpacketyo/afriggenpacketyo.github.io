Generational Wealth Disparity Underpins Sustained Consumer Spending

2026-10-12 13:23:35 EDT

American consumer spending remains robust despite economic pressures, a phenomenon economist Ed Yardeni attributes to a "G-shaped economy" where older generations, particularly baby boomers, drive resilience. Boomers possess substantial net worth from investments, retirement savings, and home equity, enabling them to maintain spending habits for themselves and their families. This contrasts sharply with younger generations facing an affordability crisis, highlighting a growing generational wealth gap. Federal Reserve data further supports this, showing a significant increase in median net worth for those aged 75 and older, while families under 35 experienced declines, with older Americans increasingly drawing on investments to support spending. 2026-10-12 12:45:06 EDT

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences recognized their work on "microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilization," which has provided a stable theoretical basis for modern stabilization policies. Their research improved upon traditional Keynesian approaches by incorporating how consumers and businesses make spending and investment decisions, offering better tools to navigate economic challenges like energy crises and sudden shifts in household consumption.
