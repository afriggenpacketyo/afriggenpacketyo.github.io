Ancient Roman Shipwreck with 2,000-Year-Old Jars Discovered Off Sicily

2026-08-09 06:00:31 EDT

Italian divers have uncovered a 2,100-year-old Roman shipwreck off Sicily, laden with hundreds of well-preserved clay amphorae, likely used for transporting wine. Hailed by Italy's Culture Minister as one of the most significant underwater archaeological discoveries in recent years, the vessel was found near Mazara del Vallo after a tip-off from local fishermen. Dating back to the 2nd-1st centuries BC, the ship's cargo primarily consisted of tall, cylindrical Dressel 1A amphorae. This remarkable find offers invaluable insights into ancient Roman trade routes, economic practices, and daily life, providing a unique and tangible connection to a distant past and enriching our understanding of maritime history.

New Study Reveals Obesity Poses Greater Cancer Risk Than Estimated

2026-08-09 05:00:00 EDT

A new study led by Hermann Brenner of the German Cancer Research Center suggests that obesity is a significantly greater cancer risk than previously understood, potentially contributing to up to 11.5% of all cancer cases. Unlike prior research that primarily used BMI, this study incorporated waist circumference and waist-to-hip ratio, recognizing abdominal fat's role in triggering chronic inflammatory metabolic states that influence cell growth. The research also addressed a methodological bias by excluding early cancer cases, revealing a clearer statistical link between excess weight and cancer. This finding offers a reason for optimism, indicating that preventing obesity could make a larger and more impactful contribution to cancer prevention efforts globally.
