UK Seasons Shifting with Fewer Frosts and More Hot Days, Met Office Reports

2026-11-25 19:08:07 EST

New analysis from the Met Office indicates a significant shift in the UK's seasons, characterized by fewer winter frosts and an increase in hot summer days. Over the past decade, the number of winter days with air frost has decreased by approximately a quarter compared to the 1961-1990 period, with southern England experiencing a reduction of about a third. Conversely, the average number of summer days reaching 25°C has more than doubled across the UK. These changes are attributed to climate change, leading to warmer winters and hotter summers, which in turn impact wildlife, agriculture, and water resources. While some sectors like soft fruit cultivation have benefited, others, such as brassica crops, have seen reduced yields. The trend suggests a continued warming of the UK's climate, with more extreme hot weather and fewer extreme cold events anticipated. This analysis aligns with the Met Office's new partnership with the BBC for weather forecasting.

Weight-Loss Drugs May Increase Need for Breast Cancer Screenings, Study Suggests

2026-11-25 19:02:07 EST

A new study presented at the Radiological Society of North America meeting suggests that women taking popular weight-loss medications like Ozempic and Wegovy may experience increased breast density on mammograms. This can lead to a higher likelihood of requiring follow-up imaging, even when no cancerous abnormalities are found. The research examined 107 women who had taken GLP-1 receptor agonists for at least a year, noting a significant rise in breast density categories for many participants. While higher density is linked to increased breast cancer risk, researchers believe the observed changes are likely due to a disproportionate loss of breast fat, making the remaining fibroglandular tissue appear denser. Although the findings are not a cause for concern for women taking these medications or a reason to skip screenings, they highlight the need for open communication between patients and their healthcare providers. Further research is needed to determine if these imaging changes affect long-term breast cancer risk.
