As governor of Massachusetts in 1812, Gerry signed a controversial redistricting bill that created a sprawling, contorted state senate district designed to benefit his Democratic-Republican Party. Despite his substantial contributions to American independence, including refusing to sign the Constitution without a Bill of Rights, his legacy is predominantly linked to this enduring political term, illustrating the often unpredictable ways historical figures are remembered and their names become embedded in language.
