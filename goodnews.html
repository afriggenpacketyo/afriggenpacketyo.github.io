The Enduring Legacy of Elbridge Gerry in the Term "Gerrymander"

2026-08-19 05:00:00 EDT

Elbridge Gerry, a lesser-known Founding Father and former Massachusetts governor, unwittingly left his name to "gerrymander," the practice of manipulating voting district boundaries for political advantage. The term originated in 1812 when Gerry signed a redistricting bill, leading a newspaper to cartoonishly depict a misshapen district as a "Gerry-mander"—a portmanteau of his name and "salamander." Despite his significant historical contributions, including signing the Declaration of Independence and serving as Vice President, this incident in state legislative politics is what keeps his name alive today. The article notes that few people today recognize Gerry, often mispronouncing his surname.
