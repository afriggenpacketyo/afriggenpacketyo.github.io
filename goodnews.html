2026-11-25 09:14:34 EST

Liberia, a West African nation founded by Black Americans in the 19th century, observes its own distinct Thanksgiving holiday on the first Thursday of November, a tradition transplanted by early settlers. The holiday has evolved into a national day of religious gratitude, unity, and harvest, officially established in 1883 following a presidential invocation for divine protection against British territorial incursions. Liberian families typically begin the day with church services before gathering for large feasts featuring local dishes such as jollof rice, various chicken preparations, and fufu with soup. This celebration highlights a unique cultural adaptation and a profound sense of thankfulness for freedom, divine protection, and peace after past civil conflicts.
