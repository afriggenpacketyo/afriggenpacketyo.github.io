US Offshore Windfarms Transform Seafloor into Artificial Reefs, Impacting Marine Ecosystems

2026-11-18 09:00:04 EST

U.S. offshore windfarms are inadvertently creating artificial reef habitats, significantly transforming stretches of the seafloor and consequently impacting marine life and the fishing industry. The submerged structures of wind turbines attract various bivalves and algae, which in turn draw in smaller creatures like crabs and fish, eventually attracting larger species such as black sea bass. Researchers are actively studying whether this "reef effect" primarily concentrates existing fish populations or genuinely increases overall numbers, a phenomenon dubbed a "huge experiment in biogeography." This ecological shift could favor reef-oriented species over those accustomed to open spaces and potentially introduce invasive species. The article also balances these impacts by emphasizing that climate change poses a far greater threat to wildlife than wind turbines.
