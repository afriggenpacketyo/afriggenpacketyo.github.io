The US Vaccine Court: Compensating Rare Vaccine Injuries While Protecting Public Health

2026-07-26 19:00:13 EDT

The United States Vaccine Injury Compensation Program (VICP), commonly known as "vaccine court," operates as a specialized legal forum established in the 1980s to provide compensation for Americans who experience rare injuries after vaccination, while simultaneously shielding vaccine manufacturers from excessive liability. This no-fault system, presided over by special masters rather than juries, requires petitioners to demonstrate that their injury was more likely than not caused by a vaccine, without needing to prove negligence. Funded by a 75-cent excise tax on recommended childhood vaccinations, the program has disbursed nearly $5 billion to approximately 12,000 individuals. The article features the compelling case of Jacob Thompson, whose severe epilepsy was linked to his six-month vaccinations, resulting in a $2.1 million judgment and a lifetime annuity for his care. Despite its vital public health role, the court currently grapples with a significant backlog of over 3,000 cases and an urgent need for additional resources from Congress.
