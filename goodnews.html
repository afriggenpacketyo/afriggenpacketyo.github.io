Louisiana is preparing to host its first recreational alligator hunt in over half a century, with up to 5,000 permits issued for a month-long season commencing on October 1. Alligator hunting was initially banned in 1962 to allow the species to recover, and populations are now robustly estimated at over 3 million. The Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries asserts that the abundant alligator resource can sustainably support recreational harvesting without negatively impacting the state's valuable commercial industry. Licensed hunters will adhere to strict rules, including hook and line trapping and non-commercial sale of meat or skin, bringing Louisiana in line with other states like Florida.
