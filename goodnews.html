Ancient teeth reveal 25,000-year-old betel nut chewing practice

2026-09-19 07:21:56 EDT

Analysis of ancient teeth found in Indonesia has provided evidence of betel nut chewing dating back as far as 25,000 years, suggesting an early stage of the practice where hunter-gatherers may have sucked on the nuts for their pain-relieving effects. Researchers observed unique grooves on the teeth, inconsistent with typical food wear, which they matched to the shape of betel nuts. Chemical analysis of the dental tissues also revealed traces of arecoline, a key chemical in betel nuts. This discovery suggests that the habit of sucking betel nuts, possibly for their numbing and pain-relieving properties, was prevalent among early human populations. The findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the long history of substance use in human culture.
