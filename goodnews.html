Autumn equinox marks astronomical start of fall in the Northern Hemisphere

2026-09-21 20:05:52 EDT

Astronomical autumn begins with the autumn equinox, occurring at 01:06 BST on September 23rd. This event signifies the moment the Sun crosses the celestial equator, resulting in roughly equal lengths of day and night across the globe. The term 'equinox' derives from Latin for 'equal night.' While the equinox itself is not visible, it holds cultural and spiritual significance for some. Due to atmospheric refraction, the actual 'equilux' (equal light) occurs a few days later, around September 25th or 26th in the UK, with daylight hours shortening noticeably after the equinox.
