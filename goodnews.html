Understanding Ranked Choice Voting as Two States Utilize System for Congressional Races

2026-10-30 16:24:38 EDT

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is being utilized in congressional and state races in Maine and Alaska, enabling voters to rank candidates by preference to ensure a majority winner. The system works by reallocating votes from eliminated candidates to voters' next choices if no one secures over 50% of first-rankings in the initial count. Proponents argue RCV reduces the "spoiler effect" of third-party candidates and encourages less negative campaigning, as candidates seek broader appeal for second and third-place votes. However, the system faces significant opposition and has been banned in 19 states, with ongoing debates regarding its effectiveness, learning curve, and susceptibility to misinformation, particularly in polarized political climates.
