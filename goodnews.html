Liechtenstein Amends Royal Succession Laws to Allow Women on Throne

Liechtenstein has announced a significant change in its royal succession rules, allowing women to ascend the throne, a declaration made on the central European country's national day. The tiny principality, with a population of 44,000, is shifting from male primogeniture to absolute primogeniture, meaning the first-born child, regardless of sex, will become heir to the throne. While this change will not affect the current ruler, Prince Hans-Adam II, or his heir, Hereditary Prince Alois, it will apply to the descendants of Prince Alois and Hereditary Princess Sophie. This move aligns Liechtenstein with other European monarchies, such as Britain, which made similar changes in 2011, ensuring future leadership is based on birth order rather than gender.
