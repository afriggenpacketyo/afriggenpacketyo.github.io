Understanding Whether Old Debt Can Reappear on Your Credit Report

2026-08-17 11:57:27 EDT

Many borrowers are concerned about old debt reappearing on their credit reports, especially with rising credit card balances. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), most negative information, including collection accounts, can remain for about seven years from the initial delinquency date. While a debt collector cannot "re-age" an account to restart this clock, new collectors may report legitimate debt if it's still within the allowable window. Borrowers should verify details, dispute inaccuracies, and understand that credit-reporting limits differ from debt's statute of limitations, which can be reset by partial payments (not investment advice).

Understanding 401(k) Protection from Debt Collectors and Garnishments

2026-08-17 11:09:26 EDT

Many Americans with rising debt are concerned about whether debt collectors can garnish their 401(k) accounts. In most cases, ordinary debt collectors cannot seize funds from employer-sponsored 401(k)s due to protections under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA). However, these protections are not absolute; exceptions exist for certain debts like child support, alimony, or federal tax debt. It's crucial to understand that withdrawing money from a 401(k) can remove these protections and incur significant taxes and penalties. Addressing debt issues proactively through consolidation or settlement is advised before they escalate (not investment advice).
