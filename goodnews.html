Nobel Prize in Economics Honors "Sticky Prices" Research for Macroeconomic Stabilization

2026-10-13 06:30:00 EDT

Robert Hall of Stanford and Michael Woodford of Columbia have been awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for their pioneering work on the "microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilization," particularly the concept of "sticky prices." This theory, illustrated with the example of an ice cream menu's fixed prices, explains how prices and wages don't adjust rapidly to changing economic conditions, leading to declines in production and employment during recessions. Their research provided crucial microfoundations for New Keynesian economics, demonstrating that even with rational individual decisions, price stickiness creates a vital role for government and central bank intervention to stabilize the economy.
