Arizona's Chiricahua Monument Designated as 64th U.S. National Park

2026-10-22 08:08:40 EDT

Arizona's Chiricahua National Monument, known among Native Americans as the Land of Standing-Up Rocks, has officially become the 64th U.S. national park. President Donald Trump signed the bipartisan legislation, elevating the 19-square-mile site located within the historical territory of the Chiricahua Apache tribes. This area, famous for its hundreds of pinnacle-shaped rock formations created by an ancient volcanic eruption, boasts a rich history spanning thousands of years of human use, including the Apache resistance against settlers and the presence of Buffalo soldiers. The new national park designation is expected to shine a national spotlight on this "hidden gem," creating opportunities to welcome more visitors, strengthen local economies, and ensure the preservation of its remarkable landscape and cultural heritage, while also safeguarding tribal access for traditional ceremonies.
