Powerful El Niño Leads to Record-Low Atlantic Hurricane Season Activity

2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

A powerful El Niño phenomenon has resulted in a record-quiet Atlantic hurricane season, with no hurricanes forming yet, despite this period typically marking the peak of the season. This enormous El Niño creates strong wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds that effectively suppress storm formation, overriding the typical hurricane-feeding effect of warm ocean waters. While Atlantic activity is at a post-1950 record low, experts caution that residents should not lower their guard, as even one catastrophic storm can occur, exemplified by Hurricane Andrew in 1992. Conversely, El Niño has significantly intensified tropical cyclone activity in the Pacific, causing substantial impacts in regions like Hawaii.
