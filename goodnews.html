Scientific Breakthrough Transforms Brussels Sprouts from Bitter to Beloved Vegetable

2026-11-22 09:28:25 EST

Brussels sprouts, historically a notoriously disliked vegetable in America, have undergone a remarkable transformation into a culinary favorite, largely due to scientific advancements that eradicated their inherent bitterness. The pivotal breakthrough came from Dutch biochemist Hans van Doorn, who, commissioned by agricultural seed company Syngenta in 1988, identified sinigrin and progoitrin as the specific chemical compounds responsible for the vegetable's unpalatable taste. By 1993, through meticulous selective breeding, van Doorn successfully bred out these bitter compounds, resulting in sweeter sprouts and a nearly quintupled increase in American consumption. This article celebrates a significant achievement in agricultural science, demonstrating how targeted genetic improvements can profoundly alter consumer preferences and popularize previously challenging foods.
