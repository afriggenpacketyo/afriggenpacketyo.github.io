Euclid Telescope Uncovers Most Distant Quasar, Offering Glimpse into Early Universe

2026-11-24 17:34:00 EST

The European Space Agency's Euclid telescope has unveiled Dún Aonghas, the most distant quasar ever observed, existing a mere 630 million years after the Big Bang. This groundbreaking discovery, published in Astronomy and Astrophysics, offers unparalleled insights into the formation and evolution of early galaxies and their central supermassive black holes. Quasars, powered by vast amounts of gas accreting into black holes, are exceptionally luminous, making them visible across billions of light-years and providing a crucial window into the universe's infancy. This finding significantly contributes to understanding how cosmic structures developed remarkably quickly, with this particular black hole already 360 million times the sun's mass.
