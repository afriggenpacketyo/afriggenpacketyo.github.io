Euclid Telescope Discovers Most Distant Quasar, Shedding Light on Early Universe

2026-11-24 17:34:00 EST

The European Space Agency's Euclid telescope has achieved a significant breakthrough, discovering J1504+6252, nicknamed Dún Aonghas, which is the most distant and oldest known quasar, formed just 630 million years after the universe-creating Big Bang. This remarkable discovery, published in Astronomy and Astrophysics, provides invaluable information about the early history of the cosmos, pushing the boundaries of previous records. Quasars, characterized as extremely bright cores of active galaxies powered by gas falling into supermassive black holes, represent a brief, luminous phase in a galaxy's life. The finding strongly supports the scenario where supermassive black holes formed remarkably quickly through the direct collapse of large gas clouds, subsequently accumulating additional matter and offering new insights into galaxy evolution.
