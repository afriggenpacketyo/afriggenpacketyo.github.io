Ancient Cave Art Suggests Early Irish Settlers Originated from Wales

2026-09-05 01:52:05 EDT

A groundbreaking discovery of ancient cave art in southwest Ireland, carbon-dated to at least 15,000 years ago, strongly suggests that the earliest known human inhabitants of Ireland likely migrated from Wales. Researchers propose that during the most recent Ice Age, when sea levels were significantly lower, a "lost land bridge" emerged, connecting modern-day Pembrokeshire with the southeastern tip of Ireland near Rosslare. This natural pathway would have offered an irresistible opportunity for hunter-gatherers to cross, predating previously accepted evidence of human presence in Ireland by approximately 5,000 years. The Lost Land Bridge Project, utilizing advanced artificial intelligence to identify these sites, indicates that this forested and grassland plateau supported abundant large herbivores, making it an attractive migration route. Researchers are optimistic about the potential for further, even older, discoveries in the region.
