Scientists Discover Novel Immune Hubs in Skull Bone Marrow, Suggesting New Brain Defense

2026-08-24 04:17:00 EDT

Researchers at Washington University School of Medicine have identified previously unknown immune "security stations" within the bone marrow of mouse skulls, suggesting a proactive role in brain defense. These lymph node-like structures responded to brain tumors more rapidly than distant lymph nodes. Disrupting these hubs accelerated tumor growth in mice, while stimulating them improved tumor rejection. While human evidence is preliminary, this discovery challenges the traditional view of the brain as "immune privileged" and opens new avenues for understanding and potentially treating brain diseases by targeting these localized immune responses without direct brain intervention.
