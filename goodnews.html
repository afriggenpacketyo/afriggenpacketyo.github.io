Strategies to combat jet lag and adjust to new time zones

2026-08-05 19:49:46 EDT

Jet lag, a common travel ailment, arises from a mismatch between an individual's internal body clock and the local time of their destination. This disruption to the body's circadian rhythms, which are synchronized by environmental cues like sunlight, can lead to sleep disturbances, fatigue, irritability, and reduced cognitive function. The master clock in the brain relies on daily cycles of light and darkness to set the body's 24-hour rhythm. When traveling across multiple time zones, this internal clock struggles to adjust quickly, causing the body to remain aligned with the original time zone. This article explains the physiological basis of jet lag and offers insights into managing its effects.

Fridge-free vaccine trials show promise in reducing waste and improving access

2026-08-05 19:14:22 EDT

UK scientists have successfully trialled a "fridge-free" version of a tetanus-diphtheria vaccine, a significant step towards reducing vaccine waste and improving global access. Currently, about half of all vaccines are wasted annually due to the strict temperature control required by the "cold chain." This new technology, inspired by resurrection plants, stabilizes vaccine components as a dry powder at room temperature. Early trials in 60 volunteers showed the fridge-free vaccine to be safe and effective, eliciting equivalent immune responses to the conventional version. Larger trials are planned, with the potential to eliminate the need for refrigeration and make vaccines more accessible in remote or resource-limited areas.
