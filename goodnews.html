Archaeologists Claim to Solve Ancient Parthenon East Pediment Puzzle

2026-11-20 15:03:00 EST

Archaeologist Vinzenz Brinkmann and his team claim to have solved the 2,500-year-old "Parthenon puzzle," reconstructing the east pediment of the ancient Greek temple. Using modern technology and clues from Roman sarcophagi depicting Athena's birth, they pieced together surviving fragments scattered across various European museums. Only about 20% of the original marble volume remains, but 3D scanning and printing allowed for a 4-meter-long model and life-size prints for an exhibition. The project highlights the ongoing dispute between Greece and Britain over the return of the Parthenon sculptures, known as the "Elgin Marbles."

New York City Lawmakers Propose Ban on Boiling Lobsters Alive

2026-11-20 13:49:03 EST

New York City lawmakers are proposing legislation to ban restaurants from boiling lobsters, crabs, and other crustaceans alive, citing recent scientific studies indicating these animals experience significant pain. The bill, sponsored by Councilmembers Oswald Feliz and Harvey Epstein, would not prohibit serving prepared crustaceans but would impose civil penalties for violations. This initiative aligns with regulations in several European countries, including Switzerland and the UK, which have already banned the practice. Proponents argue that boiling animals alive constitutes animal cruelty and that New York City has a responsibility to ensure ethical consumption practices.
