UK Homes Adapt to Climate Change with "Passivhaus" Standard

2026-08-21 19:56:48 EDT

As the UK experiences increasingly hotter summers, a significant number of homes are at growing risk of overheating, prompting innovative adaptations such as the "Passivhaus" building standard. Architect Marion Baeli's retrofitted 1960s London home, for instance, remarkably maintains temperatures significantly cooler than outside without the need for air conditioning, thanks to extensive insulation and an airtight design. This international building standard, originally developed in Germany, aims to ensure constant indoor temperatures and drastically reduce energy consumption. While retrofitting existing homes to this standard can be costly, initial Passivhaus construction is considerably cheaper, and future UK building regulations are progressively moving closer to these robust standards to effectively future-proof homes against the challenges of rising heat.
