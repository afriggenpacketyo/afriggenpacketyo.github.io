Understanding Eligibility for Medicare Supplemental Insurance (Medigap)

2026-08-10 12:42:34 EDT

Medicare supplemental insurance, or Medigap, is designed to cover certain out-of-pocket costs that Original Medicare does not, but specific eligibility and enrollment rules apply. To qualify, individuals must be enrolled in both Medicare Part A and Part B, and Medigap cannot be used in conjunction with Medicare Advantage plans. A critical six-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period begins the first month a person is 65 or older and enrolled in Part B, offering protections against denial or higher premiums due to health. For those under 65, eligibility is more complex as federal law does not require insurers to sell Medigap, though some states provide additional safeguards. (not investment advice)

Understanding Unsolicited Debt Forgiveness from Creditors

2026-08-10 11:39:04 EDT

While rare, creditors can forgive debt without being asked, though it's not a reliable strategy for borrowers. This might occur if a debt is too small to pursue, recovery is unlikely due to financial hardship, or as part of an internal policy. It's crucial not to confuse a "charge-off" with forgiveness, as creditors often continue collection efforts or sell the debt. Instead of waiting, taking a proactive approach like debt settlement, debt management plans, or consolidation loans offers more control. These options allow borrowers to address substantial unsecured debt, potentially reducing balances or interest, but come with their own risks and eligibility requirements. (not investment advice)
