Neanderthal Shoulder Bone Reveals Earliest Evidence of Wooden Spear Violence

2026-10-15 11:00:05 EDT

Archaeologists have identified a 15mm hole in a Neanderthal shoulder blade, discovered in a cave in Iraqi Kurdistan and dating back 75,000 years, which they believe represents the earliest evidence of lethal interpersonal violence from a sharpened wooden spear. The research suggests the spear likely entered the individual's chest, causing damage to vital organs before exiting through the shoulder. While acknowledging the possibility of a hunting accident, experts such as Professor Chris Stringer lean towards an attack by a rival as a more probable explanation. This discovery adds to a growing body of evidence indicating that Neanderthal societies may have experienced more violence than previously understood, offering new insights into the complex social behaviors of our extinct human relatives.
