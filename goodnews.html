Newly Discovered 'Coffee Frogs' in Costa Rica Face Threat from Pesticides

2026-08-08 15:48:58 EDT

A tiny, nocturnal greenish frog, dubbed the "spring frog" or "coffee frog," has been identified as a new species endemic to Costa Rica's central Pacific region. Measuring less than 4 centimeters, these amphibians thrive in coffee plantations where males emit distinctive high-pitched calls with translucent throats. While initially observed in the 1960s, genetic and morphological studies confirmed its unique species status in 2021. However, researchers warn that agrochemicals used in coffee farming pose a significant threat to these frogs, as pesticides can seep into aquatic systems where tadpoles develop, alongside other environmental dangers.
