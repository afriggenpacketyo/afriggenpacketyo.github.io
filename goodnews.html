Study Links Severe COVID-19 to Reactivation of Dormant Viruses, Potential Long COVID Factor

2026-08-10 19:17:48 EDT

A new study indicates that severe COVID-19 infections may trigger the reactivation of certain dormant viruses, including Epstein-Barr and herpes simplex virus-1, which typically lie inactive within the body. Researchers monitored over 1,100 hospitalized COVID-19 patients, identifying 11 different reactivated viruses within 40 days of hospital admission. Notably, the reactivation of Anelloviridae, a virus carried by most adults and previously thought harmless, showed an association with long-term physical disability and long COVID. These findings suggest that viral reactivation during severe illness might be driven by inflammation rather than solely immune suppression, potentially opening new pathways for understanding and treating long COVID, although further research is essential to confirm a causal link.
