Ancient Roman Concrete May Offer Clues to Giza Pyramid Construction and Durability

2026-09-04 02:53:47 EDT

A new study on nearly 2,000-year-old Roman concrete suggests it may hold clues to how the Giza Pyramids were constructed and have endured for millennia. An alternative scientific theory posits that the pyramid blocks were cast from an early form of concrete, a notion previously challenged by questions of durability. The research, published in Science Advances, reveals that ancient Roman concrete possesses a self-healing capability, growing stronger over centuries by forming calcite crystals that fill pores and microcracks. This remarkable durability breathes new life into the hypothesis that similar advanced materials could have been used in the pyramids.
