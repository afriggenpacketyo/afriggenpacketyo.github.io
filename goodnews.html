Study suggests high coffee consumption may negatively impact bone mineral density in postmenopausal women

2026-09-05 16:32:27 EDT

A new study from Flinders University indicates that women aged 65 and older who consume more than five cups of coffee daily may experience lower bone mineral density (BMD). Researchers analyzed data from over 9,700 women over a decade, measuring BMD in critical areas like the femoral neck and hip. While moderate coffee intake showed no adverse effects, tea consumption was linked to higher BMD. The findings suggest that dietary factors, including beverage choices, play a significant role in bone health, particularly for women facing accelerated bone loss after menopause. The study highlights osteoporosis as a growing global health concern.

The United Nations is advocating for the replacement of the prevalent Mercator map projection with one that more accurately depicts the true size of continents, particularly Africa. The Mercator map, created for navigation, significantly distorts the size of landmasses, making Africa appear similar in size to Greenland when it is actually much larger. The proposed "Equal Earth" projection aims to correct this distortion, though it sacrifices the navigational accuracy of the Mercator map. This initiative highlights how cartographic choices can influence global perceptions of regions and their importance.
