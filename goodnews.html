Study challenges narrative of higher crime rates among non-German nationals in Germany

2026-10-14 04:48:00 EDT

A new study by sociologist Susann Prätor challenges the common narrative, often promoted by the AfD party, that non-German nationals commit significantly more crimes than Germans. While official police crime statistics show a higher frequency of offenses by non-citizens, Prätor's research, which includes unreported crime data, indicates that non-Germans are reported to the police twice as often. When adjusted for this reporting bias, the difference in crime rates between the groups is significantly reduced. The study also highlights factors like age, gender, and upbringing as key influences on violent behavior, rather than culture, and emphasizes that the vast majority of both German and non-German residents do not commit serious violent crimes.
