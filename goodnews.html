Royal Pavilion in Brighton Showcases Indian Architectural Influence in England

2026-09-02 10:30:36 EDT

The Royal Pavilion in Brighton, England, stands as a stunning architectural marvel, prominently featuring Mughal arches, onion domes, and chhatri-topped towers, reflecting significant Indian influence. Constructed in the late 18th century for British royals, its design embodies a historical fascination with South Asian architecture during the era of British rule in India. Queen Victoria notably resided there before the building was transformed into a military hospital for Indian soldiers during World War I, even featuring segregated kitchens. Today, the pavilion operates as a public museum, with its gardens undergoing restoration and having hosted some of the UK's first same-sex marriages, highlighting its rich and evolving cultural significance.
