Ming Dynasty Encyclopaedia Reveals 17th-Century Chinese Worldview

2026-08-26 00:00:05 EDT

The "Tushu Bian," a 127-volume encyclopedia published in 1613, offers a profound glimpse into how Chinese scholars understood the world during the Ming dynasty. Compiled by Neo-Confucian scholar Zhang Huang, this monumental work synthesized over 200 printed sources to document concepts of heaven, Earth, and humanity. Information designer Paul Kahn highlighted its purpose: to gather all necessary illustrations and texts for an educated person to comprehend their world. Despite being too costly to print during Zhang's lifetime, its eventual publication showcased a comprehensive knowledge system, comparable to Euro-Mediterranean cultural perspectives of the era.
