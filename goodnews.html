Scientists Discover Novel Immune 'Security Stations' in Skull Bone Marrow

2026-08-24 04:17:00 EDT

Researchers have identified previously unknown immune "security stations" within the bone marrow of mouse skulls, suggesting a more active role for the skull in defending the brain. These lymph node-like structures were observed to respond to brain tumors before more distant lymph nodes. Disrupting these structures in mice with glioblastoma led to faster tumor growth and reduced survival rates. While similar immune cells have been found in human skull marrow, further research is needed to confirm if they are organized and function identically. This discovery challenges the long-held view of the brain as "immune privileged" and opens new avenues for understanding brain immunity.
