Understanding and Disputing Illegally Re-aged Debts

2026-08-06 10:42:23 EDT

This article explains that debt collectors are generally prohibited by federal law, specifically the Fair Credit Reporting Act, from "re-aging" old debts. Re-aging involves improperly changing the original date of delinquency to extend how long a debt appears on a credit report, which should typically be seven years. Borrowers must be vigilant for mismatched dates or attempts by collectors to solicit small "goodwill" payments, as even acknowledging an old debt can restart the statute of limitations in some states. Individuals have the right to dispute such discrepancies directly with credit bureaus, forcing collectors to validate the debt's true age and ownership chain. (not investment advice)
