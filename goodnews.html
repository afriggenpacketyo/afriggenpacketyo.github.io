Historical account of pedestrianism, a Victorian-era endurance sport

2026-11-03 04:30:43 EST

This article delves into the history of 'pedestrianism,' a popular Victorian-era endurance sport characterized by long-distance walking challenges. It highlights the career of Edward Payson Weston, a prominent figure who turned wagers into a professional pursuit, drawing large crowds and significant betting. The sport's culture, which presaged modern commercialized sports, involved grueling events, meticulous record-keeping, and even debates over performance-enhancing substances like cocaine. Pedestrianism's decline in the late 19th century was attributed to the rise of shorter, more accessible pastimes like bicycling and baseball. The article draws parallels between the betting and ethical concerns of pedestrianism and those in present-day sports and politics.

Chinese drug 'iza-bren' shows promise in doubling lung cancer survival window

2026-11-03 03:00:09 EST

An anticancer drug developed in China, known as iza-bren, has demonstrated significant potential in a Phase 3 clinical trial, reportedly doubling the progression-free survival window for advanced lung cancer patients. The drug, already approved for certain indications in China, was tested against platinum-based chemotherapy in a trial involving 432 patients across 73 hospitals. Researchers found that patients receiving iza-bren had a median progression-free survival of 5.7 months, compared to chemotherapy alone. This development could represent a shift in cancer treatment strategies, offering new hope for patients with advanced lung cancer. Further details on its efficacy and broader application are anticipated.
