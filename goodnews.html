Understanding and Combating Jet Lag Through Circadian Rhythm Adjustment

2026-08-05 19:49:46 EDT

Jet lag, a common and disruptive consequence of rapid travel across multiple time zones, occurs when the body's internal clock struggles to synchronize with local time. Dr. Nina Rzechorzek of Cambridge University explains that this biological mismatch leads to symptoms such as disturbed sleep, daytime sleepiness, poor concentration, irritability, and digestive problems. Dr. Anna Edmondson further elaborates that the body's master clock, primarily set by daily cycles of sunlight and darkness, requires several days to adjust to new environmental timing cues. Effective strategies to combat jet lag involve understanding these intricate circadian rhythms and actively working to reset the body's internal timing mechanisms to the new destination's schedule.
