Research Suggests Very Hot Drinks May Increase Esophageal Cancer Risk

2026-09-08 19:30:39 EDT

New UK research indicates that regularly consuming very hot beverages, such as tea and coffee, at temperatures of 65 degrees Celsius or above, may significantly increase the risk of oesophageal cancer, also known as cancer of the food pipe. This study, which involved nearly a million people, is the largest to date to establish this link, finding that individuals who drank "very hot" beverages had approximately a three times higher risk compared to those who preferred "warm" drinks, although the overall chances of developing this cancer remain low. The mechanism is believed to involve damage to the cell lining of the food pipe from the steaming hot liquid. Experts advise waiting a few minutes for drinks to cool to a comfortable temperature before consumption to mitigate this potential health risk.
