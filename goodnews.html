Atlantic Hurricane Season Reaches Record Low for Hurricane Activity, Attributed to El Niño

2026-09-15 11:44:00 EDT

The Atlantic Ocean has experienced a historically quiet hurricane season, setting a 60-year record for the longest stretch without a hurricane since modern storm tracking began in 1966. This unusual calm, with no hurricanes forming by September 15th, is primarily attributed to a strong El Niño climate pattern. El Niño typically causes warmer sea surface temperatures in the Pacific and increased vertical wind shear in the Atlantic, creating conditions unfavorable for tropical cyclone development. Forecasters had predicted below-normal activity, and this year's trend aligns with past strong El Niño events, which consistently coincided with calmer Atlantic hurricane seasons.

The discovery strongly supports theories of continental fauna exchange between North America and Europe during the Late Jurassic period, possibly via ephemeral land bridges or episodic sea regressions across the proto-Atlantic Ocean.
