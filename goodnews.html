Chile Achieves First South American Validation for Eliminating Dog-Transmitted Rabies

2026-09-14 17:49:35 EDT

Chile has been validated by the World Health Organization as the first South American country to eliminate dog-transmitted rabies to humans, a significant public health achievement spanning over five decades. The last reported case was in 1972, a testament to sustained investment in prevention and surveillance. Extensive pet vaccination campaigns, community awareness, and prompt access to post-exposure prophylaxis were crucial. This milestone offers valuable lessons globally, as human cases of dog-transmitted rabies in the Americas have dropped by over 98% since 1983, showcasing the impact of dedicated public health policies.
