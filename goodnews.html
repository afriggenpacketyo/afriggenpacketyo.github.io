Powerful El Niño Leads to Record-Low Atlantic Hurricane Season Activity

2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

A robust El Niño is driving a record-low Atlantic hurricane season, with activity currently at only 7% of normal, marking the longest period without a hurricane in the satellite era. This natural phenomenon creates a "triple whammy" of strong wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds, effectively crushing storms before they can develop, despite record-warm ocean temperatures. While the Atlantic remains unusually quiet, experts warn that a single catastrophic storm is still possible, citing past examples like Hurricane Andrew. Conversely, El Niño is significantly intensifying tropical cyclone activity in the Pacific, which has experienced nearly double the normal storm count, including two recent impacts on Hawaii.
