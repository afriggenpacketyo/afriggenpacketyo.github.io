Ming Dynasty Encyclopaedia Offers Insight into 17th-Century Chinese Worldview

2026-08-26 00:00:05 EDT

The Tushu Bian, or Compendium of Illustrations and Texts, is a monumental 127-volume Ming dynasty encyclopaedia published in 1613, offering profound insight into how Chinese scholars understood the world in the 17th century. Compiled by the Neo-Confucian scholar Zhang Huang, this comprehensive work meticulously drew on over 200 printed sources across several decades to document the intricate concepts of heaven, Earth, and humanity. Although too costly to print during his lifetime, its eventual publication aimed to consolidate all necessary knowledge for an educated person to comprehend their world. This encyclopaedia provides a unique and invaluable historical perspective, distinct from contemporary Euro-Mediterranean cultural understandings of the era.
