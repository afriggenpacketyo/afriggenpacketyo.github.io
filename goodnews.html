Study Suggests Chinese Script Evolved Like a Living Organism Through Natural Selection

2026-09-01 00:00:05 EDT

New research co-led by Xie Yonghui of Shanghai Normal University and Huang Wei of Beijing Language and Culture University suggests that Chinese characters have evolved over thousands of years akin to living organisms, driven by principles of natural selection and economy. Published in Digital Scholarship in the Humanities, the study reveals that modern simplified Chinese characters are formed by repeatedly combining a small number of core components like 日 (sun), 月 (moon), and 人 (human), with new elements invented only when existing ones proved insufficient. The evolutionary pressure favored a system where high-frequency components dominated, mirroring a linguistic tendency towards greater output with less effort. This article highlights the fascinating, organic development of one of the world's oldest writing systems, adapting from complex to simplified forms to facilitate easier writing and usage.
