Dry Eyes: A Common Yet Ignored Condition in Midlife Women, With Hormonal Links

2026-09-04 05:00:00 EDT

Dry eyes, a chronic and often underestimated condition, is highly prevalent among women in perimenopause and beyond, affecting approximately half of those in the menopausal transition years. Symptoms like irritation, redness, blurry vision, and light sensitivity, if ignored, can progressively worsen and potentially lead to corneal damage and increased risk of infection. The condition is strongly linked to declining androgen levels, which affect the oil glands in the eyelids responsible for preventing tears from drying too quickly, though estrogen shifts also play a role. Experts emphasize that dry eyes should not impact one's quality of life and strongly encourage seeking medical advice, as a wide range of treatments and lifestyle changes, including omega-3 rich diets and reduced screen time, can offer significant and lasting relief.
