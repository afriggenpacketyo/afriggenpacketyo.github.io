Scientists Discover Blood Biomarker for CTE Diagnosis in Living Patients

2026-11-13 23:00:33 EST

Scientists have made a significant breakthrough by identifying a blood biomarker, eMTBR-tau243, which shows promise for diagnosing advanced chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) in living individuals. This degenerative brain condition, prevalent in contact sports due to repeated head trauma, has historically only been diagnosable after death. Researchers at Washington University School of Medicine found elevated levels of this protein fragment, also associated with Alzheimer's, in autopsy-confirmed CTE cases, with levels increasing alongside disease severity. If validated in larger studies, this discovery could revolutionize how people at risk for CTE are monitored and facilitate trials for potential treatments, marking a crucial advancement in neurological science.
