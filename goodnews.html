Research Explores Drug to Prevent Long-Term Effects of Childhood Trauma

2026-08-14 02:22:00 EDT

New research, primarily using mice, investigates the potential of a drug to prevent the long-term effects of early-life adversity, rather than merely treating trauma after it occurs. The study found that early hardship leads to an overproduction of the protein FKBP51, which desensitizes cortisol receptors, keeping the stress response active. A compound called SAFit2, which blocks FKBP51, was administered to mothers and reached offspring via breast milk, effectively reversing the social and neurological impacts of early stress in the treated mice. While promising, this drug is still a research compound, years away from human application, and focuses on prevention rather than reversal of existing trauma.
