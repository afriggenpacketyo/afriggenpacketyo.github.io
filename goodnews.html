Powerful El Niño Leads to Record-Low Atlantic Hurricane Season Activity

2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

A powerful El Niño is contributing to a record-quiet Atlantic hurricane season, with no hurricanes formed yet this year, marking the longest such start in the satellite era. The season's overall activity is only 7% of normal, despite unusually warm ocean waters. Experts attribute this to classic El Niño effects: record-strong wind shear that tears storms apart, dry air that starves them, and downward-pushing winds that hinder formation. While the Atlantic is calm, El Niño is significantly boosting tropical cyclone activity in the Pacific, with eastern Pacific storm activity nearly double normal levels. Experts caution against complacency, however, noting that a single catastrophic storm can still occur, as demonstrated by Hurricane Andrew in 1992.
