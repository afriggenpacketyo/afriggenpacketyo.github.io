Understanding the Necessity of a Power of Attorney Alongside a Will

2026-11-24 14:02:00 EST

This article clarifies that while a will dictates asset distribution after death, a power of attorney (POA) is crucial for managing finances during one's lifetime, especially in cases of serious injury or incapacitation. A common misconception is that a will's executor can manage affairs immediately; in reality, their authority generally begins only after death. A durable POA allows a designated, trusted individual to pay bills, manage accounts, and handle taxes, preventing potential court authorization delays and expenses for family members. This article emphasizes that both documents serve distinct, vital roles in comprehensive estate planning, advising careful selection of an agent and ensuring compliance with varied state laws.
