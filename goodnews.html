Members like spy boys, flag boys, and wild men play crucial roles in scouting and clearing paths for these encounters. This tradition, believed to date back to the 1800s, honors Native Americans who sheltered enslaved Black people and provided an outlet for a community historically excluded from mainstream Mardi Gras parades, serving as a powerful expression of identity and community pride.
