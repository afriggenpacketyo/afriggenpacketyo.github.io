2026-09-19 07:21:56 EDT

A new study in Science Advances reveals ancient human teeth from Indonesia, dating back 8,000 to 25,000 years, with puzzling grooves that researchers attribute to a long-term habit of sucking betel nuts. This discovery suggests an early stage in the use of betel nuts, the world's fourth most commonly used mind-altering substance. Scientists replicated the sucking action with artificial saliva, confirming that the active chemical, arecoline, could be released and affect brain function. The findings indicate that early hunter-gatherers might have been addicted to the nut's pain-relieving effects, wearing their teeth down to the nerve.
