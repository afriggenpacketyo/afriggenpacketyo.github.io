Scientists Create Part-Human, Part-Mouse Brain for Disease Research

2026-09-16 11:53:27 EDT

US neuroscientists have achieved a significant scientific breakthrough by successfully engineering mice with functioning human cells integrated into their brains. The primary goal of this ethically scrutinized research is to create new models for testing potential treatments for psychiatric and neurodevelopmental diseases that are exclusively found in humans. This complex process involves genetically engineering mice to develop almost none of their own cerebral cortex, then implanting human brain-like organoids grown from human skin cells. These human cells not only divide but also organize themselves into the mouse's existing brain circuitry, connecting and developing functions similar to the outer layer of the mouse brain, without causing the mice to "think like humans." Researchers believe this innovative model holds transformative potential for understanding and treating complex conditions such as epilepsy, autism, and cerebral palsy.
