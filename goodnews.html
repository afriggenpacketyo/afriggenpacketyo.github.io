Chile Achieves First South American Validation for Eliminating Dog-Transmitted Rabies

2026-09-14 17:49:35 EDT

Chile has been validated by the World Health Organization (WHO) as the first South American country to eliminate dog-transmitted rabies to humans. This achievement follows more than five decades of sustained public health efforts, with the last reported case of dog-to-human rabies occurring in 1972. The WHO praised Chile's commitment, highlighting extensive pet vaccination campaigns, post-exposure prophylaxis access, and community awareness initiatives. This success offers valuable lessons for other nations, as human cases of dog-transmitted rabies in the Americas have dropped significantly since 1983, contrasting with high global mortality rates.

Byron Sher, Influential California Environmental Legislator, Dies at 98

2026-09-14 16:15:00 EDT

Byron Sher, a former Stanford law professor and California legislator renowned for authoring groundbreaking environmental laws, has died at 98. Despite disliking the typical political spotlight, Sher left an indelible mark on environmental policy, leading efforts to preserve ancient redwoods and authoring the state's first legislation in 1988 to stock greenhouse gases, which became a foundational element for later climate change combat bills. His work influenced federal clean air laws and continues to impact current environmental debates, such as the proposal to raise Shasta Dam. Sher was widely known for his personal integrity and ability to compromise, earning respect across the political spectrum.
