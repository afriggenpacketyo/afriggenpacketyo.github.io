Experts Explain Jet Lag and Offer Strategies for Adjusting Body Clocks

2026-08-05 19:49:46 EDT

Jet lag, the common disruption experienced after rapid travel across time zones, occurs when the body's internal clock, or circadian rhythm, struggles to align with the new local time. Dr. Nina Rzechorzek and Dr. Anna Edmondson of the University of Cambridge explain that the master clock in the brain, synchronized by daily light and darkness cycles, takes several days to adjust, causing disturbed sleep, irritability, and digestive issues. Practical strategies to combat jet lag involve managing exposure to sunshine and carefully adjusting meal times to help reset the body's internal rhythms. Understanding these biological mechanisms is key to mitigating the unpleasant effects of time zone changes.
