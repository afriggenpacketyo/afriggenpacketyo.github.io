Two New Species of Sea Spiders Discovered Along Canadian Pacific Coastline

2026-09-27 18:31:16 EDT

Researchers in Canada have announced the discovery of two new species of sea spiders in the shallow waters of the Salish Sea, marking the first such findings in this specific area in over a century. These newly identified marine arthropods, approximately one centimeter in size, were collected off the coasts of Vancouver and Quadra islands. One species, Callipallene pilosuspedes, is distinguished by its red eyes, triangular mouth, curved spine, and unique self-grooming ovigers. The second species, Tanystylum kiixin, named after a nearby ancient Indigenous village, possesses shorter, stockier legs and primarily uses camouflage for defense. These fascinating discoveries underscore the distinct characteristics of sea spiders, which diverge from their land-dwelling relatives in reproduction, feeding behaviors, and the absence of venom.
