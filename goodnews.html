Study suggests non-German nationals are more frequently reported for crimes, but differences diminish when accounting for unreported offenses

2026-10-14 04:48:00 EDT

A study by sociologist Susann Prätor indicates that while non-German nationals are reported for crimes more frequently than German citizens in police statistics, this difference significantly narrows when considering unreported offenses. Crimes committed by foreigners are twice as likely to appear in official statistics, which do not verify allegations. Adjusting for reporting bias reduces the crime likelihood difference to 1.7 times. The study also highlights that factors like age, gender, and upbringing play a role in criminal behavior, emphasizing that culture is not a primary driver. The research stresses that the vast majority of individuals in Germany, regardless of origin, do not commit serious violent crimes.

World's oldest shipwreck, dating back 4,700 years, discovered off Turkey's coast

2026-10-14 01:00:35 EDT

Archaeologists have discovered the world's oldest known shipwreck off the coast of Kekova Island in Antalya, Turkey, dating back 4,400 to 4,700 years to the Early Bronze Age. While the ship's wooden structure has not survived, over 30 ceramic storage pots (amphorae) were found on the seabed, some still containing residue of their original contents. The 10-meter-long trade vessel was likely carrying cargo across the Mediterranean. The age was determined by analyzing the amphorae's distinctive style, which matches pottery from known Early Bronze Age periods. The recovered artifacts will be displayed at the new Mediterranean Underwater Archaeology Museum.
