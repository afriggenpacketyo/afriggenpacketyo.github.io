2026-08-05 20:55:02 EDT

Inouye Solar Telescope in Hawaii, revealing a strange and dynamic facade with previously unseen feathery patterns. These images show ripples of instability, known as "Kelvin-Helmholtz instability," where magnetized plasma moves at different speeds, a phenomenon not observed at this level on the solar surface before. Experts Explain Jet Lag and Offer Tips for Adjustment

2026-08-05 19:49:46 EDT

Jet lag, a common and disruptive consequence of rapid travel across multiple time zones, occurs when the body's internal biological clock struggles to synchronize with the new local time. Dr. Nina Rzechorzek of Cambridge University explains that this fundamental mismatch causes a range of symptoms including disturbed sleep, daytime sleepiness, poor concentration, and irritability. Our circadian rhythms, primarily set by daily cycles of sunlight and darkness, require several days to adjust to new environmental timing cues. Understanding this biological phenomenon is the first step in developing effective strategies for travelers to mitigate its effects and adjust more quickly.
