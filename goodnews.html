Scientists Discover Previously Unknown Immune Hubs in Skull Bone Marrow

2026-08-24 04:17:00 EDT

Researchers at Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis have identified novel immune "security stations" within the bone marrow of mouse skulls, suggesting a proactive role for the skull in brain defense. These lymph node-like structures responded to brain tumors faster than distant lymph nodes, and their disruption accelerated tumor growth in mice. While the findings, published in "Nature," are currently limited to mice, similar immune cells in human skull marrow suggest comparable structures may exist. This discovery significantly alters the traditional understanding of brain immunity, potentially opening new avenues for treating brain diseases.
