Nobel Prize in Economics Honors Work on "Sticky Prices" and Macroeconomic Stabilization

2026-10-13 06:30:00 EDT

Robert Hall and Michael Woodford have been awarded the Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences for their profound contributions to the microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilization. Their groundbreaking work helped develop "New Keynesian economics," a framework that elucidates how "sticky prices"—prices and wages that do not rapidly adjust to changing economic conditions—can lead to prolonged economic slumps and persistent unemployment. This intellectual advancement highlights the crucial role of government and central banks in stabilizing the economy, even when individuals act rationally, by demonstrating how price rigidities prevent the "invisible hand" from fully restoring market equilibrium. Their insights have significantly reshaped modern macroeconomic theory and policy.
