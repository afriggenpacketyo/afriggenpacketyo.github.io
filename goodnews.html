Ancient Cave Art Discovery Links Ireland's First Humans to Wales

2026-09-05 01:52:05 EDT

A groundbreaking discovery of ancient cave art in southwest Ireland, dating back at least 15,000 years, suggests the earliest known humans in Ireland likely originated from Wales. Researchers from the Lost Land Bridge Project propose that a "lost land bridge" formed during the last Ice Age connected modern-day Pembrokeshire with southeast Ireland. This pathway would have allowed hunter-gatherers to follow large herbivores across a forested plateau. Utilizing AI to identify suitable cave sites, the team found evidence predating previous estimates of human presence in Ireland by 5,000 years, opening exciting possibilities for further archaeological exploration in the region.

UN Adopts New World Map to Accurately Represent Africa's Size

2026-09-04 20:53:11 EDT

The UN General Assembly has voted to adopt a new world map that more accurately reflects the true size of Africa, replacing the traditional Mercator projection. The "Correct the Map" resolution, sponsored by Togo and supported by 164 nations, addresses the Mercator map's distortion, which made countries near the equator appear smaller. Critics argue the 16th-century Mercator map minimized the development needs and potential of African nations. The new Equal Earth projection, already adopted by the African Union, aims to correct these perceptual biases, with proponents asserting that a fair world begins with a fair map, despite US opposition to the resolution.
