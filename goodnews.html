Understanding High-Functioning Depression and Anhedonia: The Silent Loss of Joy

2026-10-29 16:00:00 EDT

Psychiatrist Dr. Judith Joseph highlights "high-functioning depression," a condition where individuals fulfill daily obligations but experience anhedonia—a reduced capacity to feel joy or pleasure. Described as the "silent sister" of clinical depression, anhedonia often goes unrecognized until it leads to burnout or physical breakdown. Research by Joseph and colleagues indicates a correlation between high-functioning depression, increased anhedonia, and trauma, particularly among parents and caregivers. To help individuals identify and address this "existential crisis," Joseph has developed a self-assessment quiz and a "Five V's" framework focused on emotional well-being and self-care.
