Ranked Choice Voting Explained as Maine and Alaska Utilize System in Congressional Races

2026-10-30 16:24:38 EDT

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is being actively employed in congressional and state races across Maine and Alaska, a system that empowers voters to rank their preferred candidates in order. If no candidate secures over 50% of the initial first-choice votes, an instant runoff mechanism is triggered, where the candidate with the fewest top rankings is eliminated, and their votes are reallocated based on voters' subsequent preferences. Proponents argue that RCV effectively minimizes the "spoiler effect" and incentivizes less negative campaigning, ultimately leading to winners who command broader support from the electorate. However, the system faces considerable opposition, with 19 states banning the practice and Alaska voters poised to decide on its repeal, highlighting ongoing national debates over its effectiveness and potential for voter confusion.
