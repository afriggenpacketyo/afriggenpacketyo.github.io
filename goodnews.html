Clumsy Figeater Beetles Abound in Los Angeles During Peak Season

Los Angeles is experiencing a surge in figeater beetles, large metallic-green insects known for their clumsy flight and tendency to bump into people during their peak season in August. Megan Barkdull, assistant curator of entomology at the Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County, explains their size and small wings contribute to their erratic movement, dispelling myths of blindness. These beetles, also called green fruit beetles, thrive on soft-skinned fruits and decaying matter, playing a role in the ecosystem. Their increasing population in Southern California is linked to human factors like compost piles, irrigated landscapes, and fruit trees, alongside climate change.
