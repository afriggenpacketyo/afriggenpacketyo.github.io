Tommy John, Pioneering Pitcher and Namesake of Career-Saving Surgery, Dies at 83

2026-08-16 13:45:42 EDT

Tommy John, the legendary MLB pitcher whose career-saving elbow surgery became synonymous with his name, has passed away at 83. In 1974, facing a career-ending ruptured elbow ligament, John underwent a then-radical procedure by Dr. Frank Jobe, replacing the ligament with a tendon from his forearm. This groundbreaking "Tommy John surgery" not only revived his own career, allowing him to win 164 more games, but also revolutionized sports medicine, enabling hundreds of other baseball players to extend their careers. John, a four-time All-Star, became more famous for this medical innovation than his impressive 288 career wins, leaving an indelible mark on baseball and medical history.
