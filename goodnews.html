Newly Discovered "Coffee Frog" in Costa Rica Threatened by Pesticide Use

2026-08-08 15:48:58 EDT

A tiny, nocturnal "spring frog," recently identified as a new species endemic to Costa Rica, thrives in coffee plantations but faces significant threats from agrochemicals. Measuring less than four centimeters, these "coffee frogs" are known for the translucent throats of males, which inflate during their high-pitched calls. While they feed on insects in the coffee crops, pesticides used in farming can seep into aquatic systems where tadpoles develop. Other threats include waste dumping, increased tourism, and habitat loss due to protected area invasion.
