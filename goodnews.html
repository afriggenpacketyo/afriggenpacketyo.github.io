Liechtenstein Changes Royal Succession Laws to Allow Female Monarchs

2026-08-15 15:41:57 EDT

Liechtenstein has announced a significant change to its royal succession rules, now allowing women to ascend the throne, a declaration made on the national day of the central European country. The new "absolute primogeniture" order means that in the future, the first-born child, regardless of sex, will become heir to the throne and later princess or prince of Liechtenstein. This change will not affect the current heir, Hereditary Prince Alois, who was his father's first-born, but will apply to the descendants of his four children. Prince Alois stated the change aims to ensure the country and Princely House are led by the person best prepared for the responsibility, following a similar move by Britain in 2011.
