Brazil Achieves Lowest Amazon Deforestation Rate in Nearly Four Decades

2026-10-16 18:30:00 EDT

Brazil has recorded its lowest Amazon rainforest deforestation level in nearly 40 years, with 3,335 square kilometers cleared between August 2025 and July 2026. This represents a significant 42% decrease from the previous year, hailed as a "historic achievement" by Environment Minister Joao Paulo Capobianco. President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva reiterated the goal of achieving zero illegal deforestation by 2030, attributing the success to sustained government commitment, increased monitoring, and stricter penalties. This positive environmental news comes ahead of a tight runoff election, showcasing Brazil's progress in protecting the world's largest tropical rainforest.
