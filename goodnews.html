Voice Disorders Linked to Increased Dementia Risk in New Study

2026-10-04 12:39:14 EDT

New research suggests that voice disorders, including shifts in vocal pitch, volume, or clarity, could indicate a higher probability of developing dementia or cognitive impairment in the future. A comprehensive study involving 833,417 participants aged 50 and older examined this connection, revealing that those with a voice disorder and hearing loss had a 104% increased likelihood of developing such issues. Significantly, individuals with a voice disorder but no hearing loss still faced a 58% increased risk. Researchers from Drexel University College of Medicine, who published their findings in the Journal of Voice, highlight that seeking treatment for voice disorders might also benefit brain health. This is because social engagement, often hampered by communication difficulties, stimulates the brain and plays a role in warding off dementia. However, the study emphasizes that having a voice disorder does not guarantee future cognitive impairment, providing important context for these findings.
