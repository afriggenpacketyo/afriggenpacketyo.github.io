2026-09-03 11:01:41 EDT

Scientists in Cambridge have completed a detailed 3D map of a male fruit fly's brain, analyzing all 124 million nerve cell connections, following a similar mapping of a female fly two years prior. This groundbreaking research identified that while 95% of brain cells are shared, the remaining 5% drive distinct male behaviors such as aggression, courtship, and "love songs." Researchers emphasize this won't directly explain human behavior but offers a "new avenue in neuroscience" to understand how genetic differences affect brain wiring. This breakthrough could provide crucial clues for conditions like autism and schizophrenia by revealing how genes influence complex behaviors.
