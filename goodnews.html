The Atlantic hurricane season of 2026 is experiencing record-low activity, largely due to a powerful El Niño phenomenon that inhibits storm formation. This natural warming in the equatorial Pacific creates strong wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds, effectively "decapitating" tropical cyclones before they can strengthen. Despite record-warm ocean waters, El Niño's atmospheric effects are dominant, making it the quietest season in the satellite era. Experts caution, however, that even in a quiet season, a single catastrophic storm remains a possibility.
