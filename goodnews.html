Understanding England's 9-1 Numerical Grading System for GCSE Exams

2026-08-20 01:33:02 EDT

England's GCSE exams now employ a numerical grading system ranging from 9 to 1, a significant shift from the previous A-to-U letter scale introduced with the 2014 curriculum overhaul. The highest achievable grade is 9, while 1 is the lowest, and U denotes "ungraded." Although not a direct conversion, a grade 7 corresponds to the lower end of an A, and a grade 4 signifies a standard pass, aligning with the bottom of a C. This system, fully implemented by 2020, aims to provide clearer differentiation for high-achieving students and distinguish the updated, more rigorous GCSEs. Achieving a grade 4 or higher in English and maths is typically required for progression to further education.
