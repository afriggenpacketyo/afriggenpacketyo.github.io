Understanding and Overcoming Jet Lag Through Circadian Rhythm Management

2026-08-05 19:49:46 EDT

Jet lag, a common travel affliction, occurs when rapid travel across multiple time zones disrupts the body's internal clock, also known as circadian rhythms. Dr. Nina Rzechorzek and Dr. Anna Edmondson of Cambridge University explain that the brain's master clock, which is primarily synchronized by environmental cues like daily cycles in sunlight and darkness, struggles to quickly adjust to new local times. This significant mismatch between internal biological time and destination time results in various symptoms including disturbed sleep, daytime sleepiness, poor concentration, irritability, and digestive problems. Effectively, the body remains "stuck" in the previous timezone, making it crucial for travelers to understand these biological mechanisms to develop strategies to minimize the effects of jet lag and adapt more quickly.
