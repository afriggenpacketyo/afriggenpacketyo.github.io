Euclid Telescope Discovers Most Distant Quasar, Shedding Light on Early Universe

2026-11-24 17:34:00 EST

The European Space Agency's Euclid telescope has identified the most distant quasar known, J1504+6252, nicknamed Dún Aonghas, which formed just 630 million years after the Big Bang. This discovery provides crucial insights into the early history of the universe and the rapid development of supermassive black holes. Quasars are incredibly bright galactic cores powered by gas falling into a supermassive black hole, outshining their host galaxies. The finding supports theories that these black holes grew to enormous sizes remarkably quickly, forming through the direct collapse of large gas clouds and subsequent matter accumulation.
