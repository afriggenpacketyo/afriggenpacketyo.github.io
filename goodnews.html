Japan Launches Ambitious Mission to Mars' Moon Phobos for Sample Return

2026-10-19 17:42:23 EDT

Japan has launched an ambitious $350 million mission, Martian Moons Exploration (MMX), to study Mars' moons Phobos and Deimos in unprecedented detail. The mission's primary objective is to determine the origin of these moons, whether they were formed by a large impact or are captured asteroids. The MMX spacecraft will deploy a small rover onto Phobos' surface in 2029 and then collect soil samples from Phobos itself for return to Earth in 2031. This will be the first spacecraft to return samples from the Mars system. The mission utilizes a Japanese H3 rocket and includes instruments from NASA, Germany, and France. The rover is designed to land on Phobos' low-gravity surface and conduct exploration. The sample return capsule will re-enter Earth's atmosphere over Australia for recovery. Scientists anticipate that the collected samples will provide definitive answers about the moons' formation. The mission represents a significant step in planetary science and space exploration.
