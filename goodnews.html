Trinidad and Tobago's Anti-Gay Laws Overturned by UK Privy Council

2026-11-10 14:21:00 EST

The UK Privy Council, serving as Trinidad and Tobago's final court of appeal, has unanimously overturned the Caribbean nation's colonial-era laws criminalizing consensual sex between adult males. Jason Jones, a gay man who initiated the decade-long legal challenge, argued that the 1986 Sexual Offences Act violated fundamental constitutional rights to private and family life. The court ruled that these laws were not "reasonably justifiable in a society that has proper respect for the rights and freedoms of the individual." This landmark decision, a significant victory for LGBTQ+ rights, is expected to encourage similar legal challenges in other Commonwealth countries that still retain comparable discriminatory legislation.
