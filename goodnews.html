Covered call ETFs, popular among investors seeking monthly income, generate cash flow by holding equities and selling call options. While attractive for their yields, these funds present significant tax complexities that can surprise investors, particularly regarding "return of capital" distributions. These distributions reduce an investment's cost basis, deferring taxes until shares are sold, which can lead to higher capital gains tax liability later. Financial advisors recommend using tax-deferred accounts for these ETFs and suggest evaluating a fund's tax-cost ratio to understand the true after-tax returns, as the "juice isn't always worth the squeeze" for some investors (not investment advice).
