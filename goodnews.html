ESA's Euclid Telescope Discovers Most Distant Quasar, Formed Early in Universe's History

2026-11-24 17:34:00 EST

The European Space Agency's Euclid telescope has achieved a significant astronomical feat, discovering the most distant known quasar, formally named J1504+6252 and nicknamed Dún Aonghas. This ancient object formed within a mere 630 million years of the universe-forming Big Bang, approximately 13.1 billion years ago, providing invaluable insights into the cosmos's infancy. Quasars are exceptionally bright cores of active galaxies, powered by supermassive black holes that can outshine their entire host galaxies. This discovery further supports theories suggesting that supermassive black holes grew remarkably quickly in the early universe, likely through the direct collapse of large gas clouds, offering new data for understanding galaxy evolution and the mysteries of dark matter.
