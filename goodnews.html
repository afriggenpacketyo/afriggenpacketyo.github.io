Nanodrug Trials Show Promise for Permanent Cholesterol Control

2026-09-08 23:00:09 EDT

Clinical trials in the United States and China are showing promising results for a gene-editing nanodrug, CTX310, that could offer a one-and-done solution for controlling 'bad' cholesterol (LDL-C). The therapy uses tiny particles to deliver a CRISPR gene-editing tool directly into liver cells, targeting the ANGPTL3 gene, which regulates fat circulation in the blood. This approach aims to mimic a natural genetic mutation that results in extremely low cholesterol and triglycerides, significantly reducing the risk of heart disease. If successful, this innovation could replace daily statin pills for millions, marking a major breakthrough in cardiovascular health management.
