FDA Proposes First Major Update to Infant Formula Nutrient Requirements in Nearly 30 Years

2026-10-30 08:05:53 EDT

The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has proposed a new rule to update nutrient requirements for infant formula, marking the first significant revision in nearly three decades. The proposed changes would modify requirements for vitamins A and D, iodine, and manganese, while reaffirming existing standards for chloride, potassium, selenium, and sodium. The FDA also seeks public input on potentially requiring ingredients like arachidonic acid (ARA), docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), and L-carnitine, which are believed to support infant growth and brain development. This initiative, part of "Operation Stork Speed," aims to ensure babies receive high-quality nutrition and enhance public confidence in formula safety, with further phases planned for remaining nutrients and carbohydrate sources.
