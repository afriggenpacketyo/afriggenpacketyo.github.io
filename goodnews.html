2026-09-19 05:00:00 EDT

This article demystifies the Federal Reserve's crucial dual mandate: ensuring stable prices by controlling inflation and achieving maximum employment, primarily through its powerful tool of adjusting interest rates. When inflation runs high, the Fed typically raises rates to curb spending, thereby making borrowing more expensive for consumers and businesses, as evidenced by recent increases in mortgage rates. Conversely, rates are cut to stimulate economic activity during downturns.
