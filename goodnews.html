Ming Dynasty Encyclopaedia Reveals 17th-Century Chinese Worldview

2026-08-26 00:00:05 EDT

A monumental Ming dynasty encyclopaedia, the Tushu Bian, or Compendium of Illustrations and Texts, published in 1613, provides invaluable insight into how Chinese scholars perceived the world during the 17th century. Compiled by Neo-Confucian scholar Zhang Huang over several decades, this 127-volume work synthesized more than 200 printed sources to systematically document concepts spanning heaven, Earth, and humanity. Information designer Paul Kahn underscored Zhang's ambitious objective to create an all-encompassing resource for educated individuals to comprehend the world. This extensive and costly project, ultimately printed from woodblocks after Zhang's death, serves as a significant historical artifact, offering a unique contrast to Euro-Mediterranean cultural descriptions of the period.
