Nine New Deep-Sea Sponge Species Discovered Off Alaska Coast

2026-09-14 07:30:56 EDT

Scientists have identified nine previously unknown deep-sea sponge species off Alaska, significantly expanding knowledge of the region's largely unexplored marine biodiversity. These discoveries, made during NOAA Fisheries' bottom-trawl surveys and described in recent scientific papers, bring the total known sponge species in Alaska to 232. Sponges, ancient multicellular animals, are ecologically crucial as they filter seawater and create complex seafloor habitats vital for commercially important fish and crabs, and some produce compounds with potential cancer treatment applications. Despite their importance, these deep-sea communities face increasing threats from proposed deep-sea mining for critical minerals and bottom trawling, activities environmental groups warn could severely damage fragile ecosystems amid the intensifying climate crisis.
