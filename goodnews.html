2026-09-05 01:52:05 EDT

During the most recent Ice Age, a "lost land bridge" emerged, connecting modern-day Pembrokeshire to Ireland's southeast, which would have served as an accessible route for hunter-gatherers pursuing abundant wildlife. The project leveraged artificial intelligence to pinpoint these ancient locations, and experts anticipate a high potential for uncovering additional, even older, human settlements.

UN General Assembly Adopts New World Map Reflecting Africa's True Size

2026-09-04 20:53:11 EDT

The UN General Assembly has voted to replace the traditional Mercator world map with a new projection that more accurately reflects Africa's true geographical size. The "Correct the Map" resolution, sponsored by Togo and backed by 164 nations, addresses long-standing criticisms that the Mercator map, in common use since the 16th century, significantly distorts landmasses, particularly making Africa appear similar in size to Greenland despite being 14 times larger. Critics argue this cartographic distortion inadvertently minimizes the perceived development needs and potential of countries located near the equator. While the UN vote is non-binding, it signifies a notable shift towards more equitable and accurate cartographic representation within global institutions, aiming to reshape perceptions.
