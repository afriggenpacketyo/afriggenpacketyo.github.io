New Research Reveals Manx Shearwater Seabirds Experience "Flap Lag" or Jet Lag

2026-10-29 14:00:00 EDT

New research published in the journal Science indicates that Manx shearwater seabirds, common in the British Isles and related to albatrosses, experience a form of "jet lag," or "flap lag," during their long flights across the Atlantic Ocean. The study, led by University of Oxford behavioral ecologist Patrick Lewin, found that these birds become slightly disoriented as their internal clocks fall out of sync with the sun's position, which they use for navigation. GPS tracking data collected over 20 years revealed small but consistent navigational errors during their rapid return journeys to incubate their single eggs. This fascinating discovery highlights the widespread nature of circadian rhythms and navigational challenges across diverse species.
