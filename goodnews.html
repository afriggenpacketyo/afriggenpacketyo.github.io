Brazil Achieves Lowest Amazon Deforestation Rate in Nearly 40 Years

2026-10-16 18:30:00 EDT

Brazil has achieved a remarkable milestone, recording the lowest level of deforestation in the Amazon rainforest in nearly 40 years, marking a significant 42% decrease from the previous year. Data released by the Brazilian space research agency Inpe indicates that only 3,335 square kilometers were cleared between August 2025 and July 2026, the lowest figure since records began in 1988. Environment Minister Joao Paulo Capobianco lauded this as an "absolutely stratospheric drop," and President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva reiterated the nation's ambitious goal of achieving zero illegal deforestation by 2030. This historic environmental achievement is largely attributed to sustained government commitment to protecting the environment, including greater monitoring and stiffer penalties for illegal logging.
