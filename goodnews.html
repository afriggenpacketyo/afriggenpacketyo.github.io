Threatt Filling Station provided a vital haven for Black travelers on Route 66

2026-11-24 05:00:00 EST

During the Jim Crow era, the Threatt Filling Station in Oklahoma served as a crucial sanctuary for Black families traveling Route 66, offering fuel, food, and lodging when most establishments denied service. Founded by Allen Threatt Sr., the station became a community hub and a symbol of welcome in an era of segregation. Despite the eventual decline of Route 66 and the station's closure in the 1970s, the Threatt family has undertaken restoration efforts, preserving its history. The station is now on the National Register of Historic Places, with plans for future development to continue its legacy of hospitality.
