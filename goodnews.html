Newly Discovered 'Coffee Frogs' in Costa Rica Face Pesticide Threat

2026-08-08 15:48:58 EDT

A tiny, nocturnal frog species, newly identified as the "spring frog" or "coffee frog," has been discovered thriving in Costa Rica's central Pacific coffee plantations. Measuring less than 4 centimeters, these amphibians are notable for their dark green spots and males' translucent throats that inflate with high-pitched calls. While the frogs find shade, moisture, and insects within the coffee crops, their habitat is increasingly threatened by the use of pesticides. Researchers warn that these agrochemicals can seep into aquatic systems where tadpoles develop, posing a significant risk to the species' survival, alongside other environmental pressures like waste dumping and habitat loss.
