Ancient Mammal Fossil Reveals Early Evolution of Chewing and Suckling

2026-10-07 15:59:16 EDT

A remarkably intact 165-million-year-old mammal fossil, named Megacauda sungei, discovered in Inner Mongolia, is providing new insights into mammalian evolution. The chihuahua-sized chimera exhibits features resembling a modern platypus for semi-aquatic foraging, but its teeth suggest a predatory diet. Crucially, X-ray and CT scans revealed delicate hyoid bones and a roof of the mouth, indicating it was capable of both chewing and swallowing food, and suckling. This discovery pushes back the known timeline for these fundamental mammalian abilities, highlighting how diverse feeding methods contributed to early mammal diversification during the age of dinosaurs.
