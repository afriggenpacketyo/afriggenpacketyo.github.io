El Niño May Lead to Unprecedented Lack of Atlantic Hurricanes This Season

2026-10-02 19:37:13 EDT

For the first time in over 30 years, no hurricane has formed in the Atlantic throughout September, typically the most active month, raising the possibility of a season without any hurricanes. This unusual quiet is largely attributed to El Niño, which traditionally suppresses Atlantic hurricane activity while intensifying storms in the eastern North Pacific. The US National Hurricane Center had predicted a quieter-than-normal season with fewer named storms and hurricanes, a forecast that appears to be holding true. As the typical peak season has now passed and is on a downward trend, forecasters suggest a season without a single hurricane is increasingly possible, a phenomenon not definitively recorded since 1914.
