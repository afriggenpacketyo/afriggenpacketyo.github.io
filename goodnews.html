ADHD in Women Presents Distinct Symptoms and Significant Health Challenges

2026-07-26 05:00:00 EDT

This article highlights that Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) manifests differently in women than in men, often leading to delayed diagnoses and significant health risks. Girls with ADHD are more likely to appear distracted or disorganized rather than overtly hyperactive, causing symptoms to be missed for years and often misdiagnosed as anxiety or depression. The long-term consequences of untreated ADHD in women include increased problems with relationships, higher risks of abuse, teen pregnancy, self-harm, and other health issues like obesity and type 2 diabetes. Diagnosis, often occurring in midlife or during perimenopause, can be life-changing, leading to effective treatment combining medication, therapy, and lifestyle adjustments.
