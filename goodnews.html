Understanding Certificates of Deposit: A Guide for Savers

2026-11-18 08:48:06 EST

Certificates of Deposit (CDs) are a type of savings account where individuals lock away their money for a specified period in exchange for a fixed interest rate, providing predictable returns. Terms can range from as short as three months to as long as ten years, though early withdrawals typically incur penalties. Interest compounds over time, and the fixed rate allows savers to precisely calculate their potential earnings. While generally FDIC or NCUA insured up to $250,000, it is crucial for savers to compare rates across different banks, select terms they can comfortably maintain, and remain vigilant about auto-renewal risks. CDs are particularly attractive in environments with high interest rates for growing and protecting money amidst persistent inflation and elevated borrowing costs. (not investment advice)
