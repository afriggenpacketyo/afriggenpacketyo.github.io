Culling of Invasive Mule Deer Begins on Catalina Island

2026-09-09 23:23:40 EDT

The controversial culling of approximately 200 invasive mule deer on Catalina Island has officially begun, confirmed by local officials. The Catalina Island Conservancy, the private nonprofit land trust responsible for protecting most of the island's 48,000 acres, is employing contracted hunters to shoot the deer as part of a comprehensive ecosystem restoration plan. The deer, originally introduced in the 1920s for sport hunting, have caused significant environmental damage by consuming native plants, increasing erosion, and reducing vital habitats for endemic species like the Catalina Island fox. Recent legal challenges to halt the culling were denied, allowing this phased eradication process to proceed.
