Scientists in Beijing have made an unexpected discovery, finding that the city's extensive population of weeping willows and poplars, long cherished for their shade, are contributing to ozone smog pollution. Initial research, which focused on the role of human activities in generating ozone, revealed that these urban trees produce unusually high levels of isoprene, a volatile organic compound. This compound then reacts with nitrogen oxide, commonly emitted by vehicles and factories, to form ground-level ozone, a known public health hazard since the 1940s. The findings challenge conventional perceptions of urban greenery and provide crucial context for understanding and addressing Beijing's complex air quality issues.
