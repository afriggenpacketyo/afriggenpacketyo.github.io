Announced at the International Conference on High Energy Physics, this breakthrough comes after 15 years of research sifting through billions of measurements from an underground collider in Beijing. Glueballs, theoretically predicted in the 1970s, are particles made entirely of gluons, which are the massless carriers of the strong force binding atomic nuclei. This discovery resolves one of physics' greatest unsolved puzzles, marking a significant advancement in understanding fundamental forces and the structure of matter.
