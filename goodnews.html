This natural warming of the equatorial Pacific creates atmospheric conditions, including strong wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds, that effectively suppress tropical storm development. Despite record-warm ocean waters, El Niño's influence has proven dominant, leading to only five weak tropical storms this year. Experts warn against complacency, however, reminding that it only takes one major storm to cause catastrophe, as exemplified by Hurricane Andrew in 1992.
