Nobel Prize in Economics Honors Work on "Sticky Prices" and Macroeconomic Stabilization

2026-10-13 06:30:00 EDT

Robert Hall and Michael Woodford have been awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for their work on "microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilization," including the concept of "sticky prices." This theory explains how prices, like ice cream prices set for a season, don't adjust quickly to economic changes, leading to significant consequences for the economy. Their research helped revive Keynesian economics by showing that even with rational individual decisions, price rigidity can keep economies in slumps, justifying government and central bank intervention. Their models are crucial for understanding and combating recessions and inflation. (not investment advice)
