Understanding and Managing Seasonal Affective Disorder

2026-10-31 05:01:00 EDT

Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD), affecting about 1 in 20 people in the U.S., manifests as sluggishness, depression, oversleeping, and weight gain during colder, darker months. Psychiatrist Dr. Norman Rosenthal coined the term in 1984, noting its predictable seasonal onset and reversal. The article provides comprehensive strategies for managing SAD, including recognizing its incremental symptoms and using light therapy, often with 10,000 lux SAD lamps, preferably in the morning. Additionally, maintaining consistent eating patterns, incorporating regular exercise to boost energy, and actively reducing stress through various methods are crucial. While these tips can greatly improve well-being, the article notes that medication may be effective if symptoms persist or interfere with daily life.
