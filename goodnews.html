Powerful El Niño Leads to Record-Low Atlantic Hurricane Season Activity

2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

A powerful El Niño phenomenon is causing a record-quiet Atlantic hurricane season, effectively suppressing storm formation despite unusually warm ocean waters. This year marks the longest period without a hurricane in the satellite era, with overall activity at only 7% of normal. Experts attribute this to a "triple whammy" of record-strong wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds—classic El Niño effects that tear apart developing storms before they can strengthen. While this El Niño traditionally dampens Atlantic activity, it simultaneously fuels a highly active Pacific hurricane season, leading to nearly double the normal storm activity there. Forecasters warn that next year could see increased Atlantic storm activity as the El Niño winds down and ocean temperatures remain elevated.
