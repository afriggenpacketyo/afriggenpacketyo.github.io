Singapore Develops World's Most Accurate Atomic Clock

2026-10-02 04:00:11 EDT

Singaporean scientists have constructed the world's most accurate clock, utilizing a single ion of the rare earth metal lutetium. This groundbreaking device boasts an astonishing precision, designed to lose only one second every 260 billion years, a timeframe nearly 20 times the age of the universe. This achievement significantly surpasses previous records, being about four times more accurate than a calcium-ion clock from China and also exceeding an aluminum-ion clock unveiled by the US National Institute of Standards and Technology. The article highlights Singapore's remarkable leap in scientific innovation, positioning it at the forefront of global metrology and technological advancement.

Orionid Meteor Shower Active Until November 7th, Peaks October 21-22

2026-10-02 03:16:45 EDT

The Orionid meteor shower, one of the best-known celestial displays, is currently active until November 7th, with its peak occurring overnight from October 21 to 22. These particularly fast meteors, entering the atmosphere at around 41 miles per second, originate from dust and debris left by Halley's Comet, which was last seen in 1986 and will reappear in 2061. Earth encounters this comet's material twice annually, also causing the Eta Aquariid shower in May. Stargazers are advised to find a dark location away from city lights, allow eyes to adjust, and observe with the naked eye for the best chance to see 10-20 meteors per hour.
