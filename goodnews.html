Understanding and Managing Seasonal Affective Disorder

2026-10-31 05:01:00 EDT

Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD), affecting about 1 in 20 people in the U.S., is characterized by symptoms like sluggishness, oversleeping, and depression during colder, darker months, typically lasting 40% of the year. Psychiatrist Dr. Norman Rosenthal, who coined the term in 1984, observed the condition reversing in spring. This article outlines five key strategies for managing SAD, including utilizing SAD lamps for intense light exposure, being thoughtful and consistent about eating times, making time for physical movement, and actively reducing stress. These proactive steps, or even medication if needed, can significantly alleviate symptoms and improve overall well-being.
