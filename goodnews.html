The Brain's Default Mode Network: Why Moments of Boredom Are Essential

2026-11-11 01:57:00 EST

The brain's default mode network (DMN) is highly active during periods of apparent idleness, such as daydreaming, playing a crucial role in memory consolidation, self-reflection, future imagining, and connecting ideas. Discovered accidentally in the 1990s, the DMN helps build an internal narrative and sense of self, interacting with the salience network to shift attention. While smartphones may reduce DMN time, direct evidence of harm is limited, though problematic use links to stress and sleep disruption, which do impact DMN connectivity. Embracing boredom can protect this vital brain function.
