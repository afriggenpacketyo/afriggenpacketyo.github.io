2026-10-30 11:09:15 EDT

A rose-colored meteorite, named Teghaza 001, discovered in the Sahara Desert in 2022, has been identified by NASA as a 4.1-billion-year-old fragment of Mars. This nearly two-pound meteorite is one of only three known ancient Martian meteorites on Earth, offering a rare glimpse into the planet's early crust and formation. Its unique mix of volcanic minerals, including zircon crystals, matches samples observed in Mars' southern highlands. Age-dating revealed the rock was altered by Martian water approximately 4.1 billion years ago, suggesting a more dynamic early water cycle than previously believed. Scientists consider this article a "Rosetta Stone for Mars," promising further research into the Red Planet's history.
