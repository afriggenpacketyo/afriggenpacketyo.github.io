Vietnam Reduces Death Penalty Offenses, Signifying a Shift in Justice Philosophy

2026-11-26 10:19:00 EST

Vietnam's National Assembly has approved a revised Penal Code, dramatically reducing the number of crimes punishable by death from 44 in 1997 to just four, effective next March. This significant reform abolishes capital punishment for six offenses, including drug trafficking and sexual assault of a person under 16, reflecting humanitarian considerations, the need for proportionate sentencing, and Vietnam's increasing international integration. While the authoritarian state has not indicated a full abolition, this "retentionist-but-reductionist" approach modernizes its legal system and aligns with a broader, albeit uneven, shift away from capital punishment seen across Southeast Asia. The European Union has welcomed this positive development, viewing it as a step towards a potential moratorium and eventual abolition.
