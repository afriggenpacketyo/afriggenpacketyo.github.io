Understanding and Overcoming Jet Lag Through Circadian Rhythm Adjustment

2026-08-05 19:49:46 EDT

Jet lag, a common consequence of rapid travel across multiple time zones, occurs when the body's internal clock struggles to align with the new local time. Dr. Nina Rzechorzek and Dr. Anna Edmondson of Cambridge University explain that this mismatch disrupts circadian rhythms, leading to symptoms like disturbed sleep, daytime sleepiness, poor concentration, irritability, reduced performance, and digestive problems. The master clock in the brain, synchronized primarily by daily cycles in sunlight and darkness, takes several days to adjust to new environmental timing cues. This article provides valuable context on the biological mechanisms behind jet lag, emphasizing that understanding these rhythms is key to effectively managing and overcoming its effects.
