United Nations Endorses New World Map Reflecting Africa's True Size

2026-09-04 18:26:35 EDT

The United Nations General Assembly has overwhelmingly approved a resolution advocating for a new world map that accurately represents the relative size of Africa and other regions. Introduced by Togo on behalf of the African Group, the "Correct the Map" resolution encourages the adoption of equal-area projections like Equal Earth, which correct the distortions of the traditional Mercator projection. While not legally binding, the resolution seeks to challenge long-standing misconceptions about Africa's scale and importance in global perception. The United States was the sole country to vote against the measure, citing concerns about an ideological agenda distracting from more pressing issues.

USL Ratifies New Collective Bargaining Agreement, Boosting Player Salaries

2026-09-04 17:37:14 EDT

The United Soccer League and USL Players Association have ratified a new collective bargaining agreement (CBA) for two levels of the organization's pyramid, effective through 2030. The agreement introduces 12-month contracts for players, a first in lower-division U.S. soccer, and significantly raises base compensation. Championship players will see their base compensation increase to $42,000 in 2026, up from $31,000, with further annual increases. The upcoming USL Premier league will launch in 2028 with a compensation floor of $67,500. This landmark agreement also includes improved medical standards, expanded healthcare, and competitive prize money, marking a significant step forward for player stability and professional standards in American soccer.

Los Angeles Renames Pershing Square After Biddy Mason, Pioneering Black Landowner

2026-09-04 16:43:59 EDT

The Los Angeles City Council has unanimously voted to rename Pershing Square as Biddy Mason Park, honoring Bridget “Biddy” Mason, a woman born into slavery who became a wealthy landowner and a founder of the city’s First African Methodist Episcopal Church. This decision reflects a commitment to valuing those who built the city and recognizes Mason's resilience and historical significance. Originally dedicated in 1866 as La Plaza Abaja and later renamed Pershing Square, the five-acre plaza will now commemorate Mason, who won her freedom in court in 1856 and set a precedent for freedom in California. While some preservationists criticized renaming a World War I memorial, supporters emphasize Mason's direct connection to the area.
