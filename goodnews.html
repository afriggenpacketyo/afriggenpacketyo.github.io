The Physics of "Cat-Turning": From Maxwell's Experiments to Modern Models

2026-09-03 21:00:10 EDT

The remarkable ability of cats to consistently land on their feet, a phenomenon dubbed "cat-turning," has captivated scientific minds for centuries, notably intriguing 19th-century mathematical physicists Sir George Gabriel Stokes and James Clerk Maxwell. Their pioneering experiments, though constrained by available technology, established foundational insights into this complex maneuver. High-speed photography later revolutionized the field, meticulously documenting the precise sequence of actions cats employ to reorient themselves mid-air. Modern research continues to refine intricate dynamical models, transitioning from idealized components to incorporating measured properties of a real cat's spine, illustrating the enduring scientific appeal of this seemingly simple feat.
