Powerful El Niño Contributes to Record-Low Atlantic Hurricane Season Activity

2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

The Atlantic hurricane season is experiencing unprecedented quietness, with no hurricanes forming yet, primarily attributed to a robust El Niño phenomenon. This natural warming event in the equatorial Pacific creates atmospheric conditions highly unfavorable for storm development, characterized by strong wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds that effectively dismantle nascent storms. Despite this record-low activity, experts caution that a single powerful storm can still be catastrophic, referencing Hurricane Andrew's late-season devastation in 1992. Conversely, the eastern and central Pacific regions are witnessing significantly increased tropical cyclone activity, with Hawaii recently impacted by two hurricanes, illustrating El Niño's global weather warping effects.
