California Scientist Unearths Remarkably Preserved 10,000-Year-Old Mastodon Tooth

2026-09-12 16:34:58 EDT

Brigid Lynch, a geomorphologist in California, made a significant paleontological discovery by finding an exceptionally well-preserved 10,000-year-old Pacific mastodon molar at a preserve in San Mateo county. Paleontologists later confirmed the find, hailing it as the most complete and spectacular mastodon molar ever seen from the region, offering a crucial new piece to the Ice Age narrative. Pacific mastodons, ancient relatives of modern elephants, were prevalent in the American West but went extinct over 10,000 years ago, primarily due to climate change impacts on their habitat and overhunting by humans. The fossilized tooth has been donated to Stanford University, where researchers will employ carbon dating to determine its precise age, further contributing to our understanding of past biodiversity and informing future ecological conservation efforts.

Powerful El Niño Contributes to Record-Low Atlantic Hurricane Season Activity

2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

A potent El Niño phenomenon is significantly contributing to a record-quiet Atlantic hurricane season, actively suppressing storm formation through a combination of exceptionally strong wind shear, dry atmospheric conditions, and downward-pushing winds. This year now holds the record for the longest period without a hurricane in the satellite era, with overall storm activity registering at a mere 7% of normal levels, surprisingly, despite record-warm ocean waters. Experts, however, caution against any complacency, emphasizing that even during an El Niño, a single catastrophic storm can still emerge, as tragically demonstrated by Hurricane Andrew in 1992. The article explains how El Niño's powerful atmospheric effects are currently overriding the hurricane-fueling warm water conditions, while also forecasting increased Pacific storm activity and potentially a busier Atlantic season next year as the El Niño weakens.
