Understanding the Necessity of a Power of Attorney Alongside a Will

2026-11-24 14:02:20 EST

This article clarifies that possessing both a will and a power of attorney is generally essential for comprehensive estate planning, as they address distinct life stages. A will primarily governs asset distribution posthumously, whereas a power of attorney grants a designated agent authority to manage financial affairs while the individual is still alive, particularly if they become incapacitated. A durable financial power of attorney ensures that responsibilities like bill payments, account management, and tax filings can be handled without court intervention, preventing delays and stress for family members. Therefore, neither document replaces the other, and establishing both provides continuous financial protection and clear guidance for loved ones.
