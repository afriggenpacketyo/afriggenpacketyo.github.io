ADHD Manifests Differently in Women, Posing Unique Health Risks

2026-07-26 05:00:00 EDT

Research indicates that Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) often presents differently in girls and women compared to boys, leading to delayed diagnoses, often not until adulthood. Girls are more likely to exhibit distraction and disorganization rather than overt hyperactivity, causing symptoms to be overlooked or misdiagnosed as anxiety or depression. This delay in diagnosis carries significant health costs for women, including higher risks for social difficulties, abuse, teen pregnancy, self-harm, obesity, eating disorders, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. Emerging evidence also suggests perimenopause can exacerbate ADHD symptoms, making timely recognition and comprehensive treatment crucial for improving women's long-term well-being.

Indigenous Children Learn Native Languages with AI-Powered SkoBot Robots

2026-07-26 05:00:00 EDT

Danielle Boyer, a member of the Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa Indians, invented SkoBot, a customizable robot designed to help Indigenous children learn endangered Native languages like Anishinaabemowin. Inspired by talking toys, these small, Star Wars-like robots use a form of AI to play prerecorded audio files in response to questions, avoiding generative AI's potential for misinformation. Boyer's nonprofit mentors children in building and programming their own SkoBots, fostering interest in language, science, math, and ethical AI use. This initiative aims to revitalize cultural heritage and combat language loss, a legacy of U.S. Indian boarding schools, by making learning engaging and relevant for youth.
