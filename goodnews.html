This finding challenges long-held assumptions, including those from Charles Darwin, that flightlessness in birds only emerged as a secondary adaptation after species had already mastered aerial capabilities. The discovery suggests that early bird adaptation was more diverse than previously thought, branching into terrestrial, non-flying niches much earlier in evolutionary history. This research, published in Nature, indicates that powered flight was not the sole driver of early avialan skeletal modifications.
