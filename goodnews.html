Novel Immune "Security Stations" Discovered in Mouse Skull Bone Marrow

2026-08-24 04:17:00 EDT

Researchers at Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis have identified previously unknown immune "security stations" within the bone marrow of mouse skulls. These lymph node-like structures responded to brain tumors more rapidly than distant lymph nodes, and their disruption accelerated tumor growth in mice. This discovery challenges the traditional view of the brain as "immune privileged" and suggests the skull plays an active role in brain defense. While currently demonstrated in mice, similar immune cells in human skull marrow suggest potential for related structures, offering new avenues for brain disease treatment.
