Ancient Mastodon Molar Discovered by Scientist in California

2026-09-12 16:34:58 EDT

A remarkably preserved 10,000-year-old Pacific mastodon molar has been discovered by geomorphologist Brigid Lynch at a preserve in San Mateo County, California. Paleontologists hailed the find as the most complete and spectacular mastodon molar from the region, offering invaluable insight into the Ice Age. Pacific mastodons, ancient relatives of elephants, were prominent in the American West but went extinct over 10,000 years ago, a demise linked to climate change and human hunting. The fossilized tooth has been donated to Stanford University, where researchers will employ carbon dating to determine its exact age and study it to better understand past biodiversity and inform future ecosystem decisions.

Powerful El Niño Leads to Record-Low Atlantic Hurricane Season Activity

2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

A powerful El Niño phenomenon is significantly contributing to a record-low Atlantic hurricane season, actively suppressing storm formation despite unusually warm ocean temperatures. This year has set a record for the longest period without a named hurricane in the satellite era, with overall activity at a mere 7% of normal. Experts attribute this suppression to El Niño's "triple whammy" effect: strong wind shear dismantling developing storms, dry air depriving them of moisture, and downward-pushing winds hindering their growth. Conversely, the eastern and central Pacific have experienced double and 40% more tropical cyclone activity than usual. Despite the current calm, forecasters emphasize that a single powerful storm can still cause catastrophic damage.
