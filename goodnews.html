Historic Threatt Filling Station on Route 66 Honored for Welcoming Black Travelers

2026-11-24 05:00:00 EST

During the Jim Crow Era, the Threatt Filling Station on Route 66 in Oklahoma was a rare sanctuary for Black travelers, offering fuel, food, and lodging when most other establishments denied them service. Founded in 1915 by Allen Threatt Sr., a descendant of formerly enslaved people, this Black-owned and operated business became a vital community hub between "sundown towns." Now on the National Register of Historic Places, the station, which closed in the 1970s, is undergoing restoration by Threatt's grandchildren. The family plans to expand it into an RV park and restaurant, preserving its legacy of welcome and resilience.

Colombia Mobilizes to Protect Frailejons, Vital "Water Guardians" of Andean Wetlands

2026-11-24 05:00:00 EST

Colombia is intensifying efforts to protect frailejons, unique fuzzy plants vital for the country's water supply in the high Andean wetlands known as páramos. These sunflower relatives absorb water from cloud cover, maintaining moisture for major rivers, but two-thirds of their nearly 150 species are at risk from farming, ranching, coal mining, and climate change. Local initiatives, like Moisés Moreno's greenhouse and reforestation expeditions, are crucial for their survival. A popular cartoon character, Ernesto Pérez, has also raised public awareness, inspiring volunteers to help replant these "sacred guardians of the water" and safeguard Colombia's fragile ecosystems.
