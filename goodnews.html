Denisovan Fossils in China Reveal Ancient 'Hunting Paradise' Adapted to Local Resources

2026-09-12 22:00:10 EDT

New research on Denisovan fossils found in China suggests that this archaic human group inhabited a "hunting paradise" with a stable subsistence pattern highly adapted to local resources. The findings, published in Nature, indicate that the Bianfu Cave in southwest China is a significant Denisovan site, yielding the most abundant remains of the hominin group outside of Siberia. Denisovans, who diverged from modern humans and Neanderthals hundreds of thousands of years ago, remain largely enigmatic due to limited fossil evidence. This discovery provides crucial insights into their lifestyle, diet, and geographical distribution within Eurasia's diverse environments.
