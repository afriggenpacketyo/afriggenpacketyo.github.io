Noah Adams, Longtime Host of NPR's All Things Considered, Passes Away at 84

2026-10-18 12:30:00 EDT

Noah Adams, a distinguished writer, broadcaster, and storyteller, who hosted NPR's "All Things Considered" for 18 years, has died at 84. Adams was known for guiding listeners through major historical events and for his empathetic, detailed reporting on everyday life, particularly in rural America. Colleagues praised his "unbelievable powers of observation" and ability to connect with people, often steering away from official narratives. He authored five books and his 1981 documentary "Father Cares: The Last of Jonestown" was particularly influential, inspiring figures like Ira Glass. Adams helped shape NPR's early identity, advocating for capturing the country's diverse voices and stories beyond centers of power.
