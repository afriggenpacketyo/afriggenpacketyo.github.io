ESA's Euclid Telescope Discovers Most Distant Quasar, Offering Insights into Early Universe

2026-11-24 17:34:00 EST

The European Space Agency's Euclid telescope has revealed Dún Aonghas, the most distant known quasar, which formed within 630 million years of the universe-forming Big Bang, setting a new record. This extremely bright core of an active galaxy is powered by gas falling into a supermassive black hole, already grown to about 360 million times the mass of the sun at such an early cosmic stage. The discovery provides invaluable insights into the early history of the universe, supporting scenarios where supermassive black holes form rapidly through the direct collapse of large gas clouds. Studying such quasars helps astronomers understand the formation and evolution of the first galaxies and their central black holes.
