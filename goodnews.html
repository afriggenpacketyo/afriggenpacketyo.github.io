The small Canadian town of Gander, Newfoundland, became an unexpected haven for 7,000 international travelers after US airspace closed on September 11, 2001. With 38 planes diverted to its airport, the town's 12,000 residents mobilized, turning churches, schools, and even a hockey rink into shelters. Former Mayor Claude Elliott and volunteer Beulah Cooper recount the outpouring of generosity, providing food, comfort, and homes to the "plane people."
