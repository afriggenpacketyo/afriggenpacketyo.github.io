Newly Discovered 'Coffee Frogs' in Costa Rica Face Threat from Pesticides

2026-08-08 15:48:58 EDT

A new species of tiny, nocturnal tree frog, nicknamed the "coffee frog," has been discovered in Costa Rica's central Pacific region, thriving in coffee plantations. Measuring less than 4 centimeters, these greenish amphibians are endemic to Costa Rica and are distinguished by the males' translucent throats and high-pitched calls. However, researchers warn that pesticide use on these plantations poses a significant threat to the frogs, as the chemicals can seep into aquatic systems where tadpoles develop, endangering the species alongside other environmental factors.
