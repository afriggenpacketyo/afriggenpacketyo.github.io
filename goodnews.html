The cloak was originally a farewell gift from Māori leaders in 1900 to Dr. George Cleghorn, a medical doctor who had worked in New Zealand, and was subsequently loaned to the museum in 1913 by his widow. The repatriation was funded by The Homebound Project, a charity dedicated to returning cultural artifacts, which described the cloak as "crying to come home." This event signifies a positive step in cultural heritage preservation and reconciliation between institutions and indigenous communities.
