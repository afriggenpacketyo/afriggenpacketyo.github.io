Study Links Mysterious Grooves on Ancient Teeth to Betel Nut Sucking

2026-09-19 07:21:56 EDT

Scientists have uncovered mysterious, deep grooves on ancient human teeth from Indonesia, dating back 25,000 years, which they now link to the practice of sucking betel nuts. This "chance archaeological discovery," published in *Science Advances*, suggests an early stage in the use of betel nuts, possibly for their painkilling effects. Researchers used artificial saliva and frog egg cells to demonstrate that sucking the nuts releases arecoline, an active chemical. The theory, supported by traces of arecoline in the dental tissues, proposes that hunter-gatherers became addicted to the numbing sensation, highlighting humanity's long history of experimenting with mind-altering substances.
