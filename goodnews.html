Ranked Choice Voting Explained Amidst Ongoing Debates and Bans

2026-11-03 20:46:42 EST

Ranked choice voting (RCV) is used in congressional and state races in Maine and Alaska, allowing voters to rank candidates by preference. If no candidate secures a majority of first-choice votes, an instant runoff eliminates the lowest-ranked candidate, transferring votes until a majority winner emerges. Proponents argue RCV minimizes "spoiler" candidates and encourages less negative campaigning, leading to more representative victors. However, the system faces opposition, with 19 states banning it, fueled by polarization and misinformation about its effectiveness, highlighting ongoing debates about electoral reform and its impact on political outcomes.
