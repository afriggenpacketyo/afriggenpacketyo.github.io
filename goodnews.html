Belgian Physicist Francis Halzen Awarded Nobel Prize for Neutrino Observatory Work

2026-10-06 07:24:19 EDT

Professor Francis Halzen, a Belgian physicist, has been awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his foundational leadership in developing the IceCube Neutrino Observatory at the South Pole. This innovative observatory uses a cubic kilometer of Antarctic ice embedded with light sensors to detect high-energy neutrinos, often called "ghost particles," which originate from violent cosmic processes in distant galaxies. Halzen's pioneering work has opened a new window into astronomy, enabling scientists to investigate extreme conditions in the universe that conventional telescopes cannot observe. His tenacity and scientific vision were crucial in realizing this ambitious project.
