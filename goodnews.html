Homeowners Tap Home Equity Lines of Credit at Highest Rate Since 2005

2026-11-10 16:37:43 EST

A new Federal Reserve Bank of New York report indicates that homeowners accessed their home equity lines of credit (HELOCs) at the highest rate in over two decades during the third quarter. Balances on HELOCs increased by $23 billion, marking the largest jump since 2005. This trend is attributed to the 'mortgage rate lock-in effect,' where homeowners with low existing mortgage rates are reluctant to sell and buy at current higher rates. HELOCs offer a way to borrow against home equity without refinancing. Despite the surge in borrowing, homeowners collectively hold substantial home equity. Experts advise caution, noting that HELOCs often have variable interest rates and that payments can significantly increase when the repayment period begins. The article emphasizes the importance of careful budgeting and considering the purpose of the borrowed funds.
