Denisovan Fossils in China Reveal Ancient 'Hunting Paradise'

2026-09-12 22:00:10 EDT

New research, detailed in two papers published simultaneously in Nature, has identified Bianfu Cave in southwest China as a significant Denisovan locality. This site has yielded the most abundant remains of this extinct human group outside the original Denisova Cave in Siberia. The findings suggest that Denisovans, who diverged from modern humans and Neanderthals several hundred thousand years ago, established a stable subsistence pattern within the region's coniferous forests and forest-steppe environments. They achieved this through specialized hunting and intensive exploitation of local animal resources, painting a picture of an ancient "hunting paradise" and greatly expanding our limited understanding of this archaic human species' life and adaptations.

Autumn Night Sky Offers Five Celestial Spectacles

2026-09-12 19:53:07 EDT

Autumn brings longer nights to the Northern Hemisphere, offering excellent opportunities for stargazing enthusiasts to observe several captivating celestial events. The season commences with the increasing prominence of the Pleiades, also known as the Seven Sisters, a bright star cluster visible to the naked eye in the constellation Taurus. Late October will feature the impressive Orionid meteor shower, created by dust trails from Halley's Comet, followed by a rare planetary parade of Mercury, Venus, Mars, and Jupiter visible before sunrise around November 14. The series of spectacles concludes with November's full Beaver Moon, which will also be a supermoon, appearing slightly larger and brighter due to its relative closeness to Earth.
