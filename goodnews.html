New Denisovan Fossil Discoveries in China Shed Light on Ancient Human Hunting Practices

2026-09-12 22:00:10 EDT

Recent discoveries of Denisovan fossils in China's Bianfu Cave are providing unprecedented insights into this extinct archaic human group. Published in Nature, two papers co-authored by researchers from institutions including the Chinese Academy of Sciences reveal that Denisovans established a stable subsistence pattern adapted to local resources. They engaged in specialized hunting and intensive animal exploitation within the region's coniferous forests and forest-steppe environments. These findings establish Bianfu Cave as the most abundant Denisovan locality outside Siberia's Denisova Cave, significantly expanding scientific understanding of the species.

The condition, affecting nearly one in four people, can lead to severe jaw ache, headaches, and tooth damage. Experts explain that stress triggers a "fight or flight" response, causing jaw muscles to tense and grind as a release mechanism. While mouthguards offer protection, addressing underlying causes like obstructive sleep apnea or anxiety through multidisciplinary approaches, including therapies and lifestyle changes, is crucial for effective treatment. Some argue that jaw movement also stimulates saliva production and cognition.
