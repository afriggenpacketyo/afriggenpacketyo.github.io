Trump and Xi's "Gung-Ho" Reference Traced to Chinese Origins

2026-09-25 06:38:11 EDT

During a significant White House meeting, President Trump's reference to the phrase "gung-ho" as Chinese prompted an examination of its true origins. The term, transliterated as "gong he" (工合), is a shortened form of "gongye hezuoshe" (工业合作社), which translates to "industrial cooperatives." These were essential grassroots labor organizations founded in China in 1938, primarily to manufacture industrial supplies crucial for the war effort against Japan. Understanding this linguistic connection provides a unique historical and cultural context to the diplomatic interactions between the two global leaders, revealing a surprising thread in their shared history.

Agrivoltaics: Combining Solar Energy and Agriculture for Dual Climate Benefits

2026-09-25 05:31:00 EDT

Agrivoltaics, the practice of co-locating solar panels with agriculture, is gaining traction as a "double climate win." This strategy, which involves growing crops or grazing livestock under solar panels, offers benefits such as crop protection from elements, water diversion, and cooler microclimates that boost yields. Additionally, cooler panels operate more efficiently. Despite its enormous potential for climate mitigation and adaptation, especially in arid regions, adoption has been slow, with agrivoltaics representing less than 1% of global solar capacity. Challenges include high costs and varying effectiveness depending on climate, but it helps bridge the cultural divide between farmers and solar developers.
