Exploring the Traditions and Showdowns of New Orleans Mardi Gras Indians

2026-08-16 19:00:28 EDT

New Orleans' Mardi Gras Indians, or Black Masking Indians, parade in elaborate, meticulously crafted suits covered in beads, feathers, and rhinestones. These suits, often most detailed for the "big chiefs," are prepared year-round for a unique form of cultural expression and mock battle. Tribes navigate the streets without a set route, aiming to confront other tribes in a "prettiest" competition, supported by spy boys, flag boys, and wild men who clear paths and communicate. This tradition, believed to date back to the 1800s, originated as a sign of respect for Native Americans who sheltered enslaved Black people and provided an outlet for a community historically excluded from main parade routes.
