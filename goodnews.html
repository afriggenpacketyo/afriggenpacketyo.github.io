Electric Vehicles Linked to Increased Motion Sickness in Some Passengers

2026-10-02 22:36:19 EDT

A growing number of passengers are reporting increased motion sickness when traveling in electric vehicles (EVs), an experience unfamiliar to them in traditional cars. Experts suggest this phenomenon stems from a "mismatch in sensory cues," as EVs lack the auditory and vibrational feedback from engines that humans associate with movement. Regenerative braking, which causes sudden decelerations if not applied smoothly, is also cited as a contributing factor, making passengers feel like they are constantly rocking. While not universal, with many EV owners reporting no issues, carmakers are exploring solutions like smoothing acceleration and adding subtle seat vibrations to address this emerging concern as EV adoption continues to rise.
