Māori Kiwi Feather Cloak Returned to New Zealand by British Museum in Ceremony

2026-10-10 15:37:20 EDT

A deeply significant Māori kiwi feather cloak, known as a kahu kiwi, has been ceremonially repatriated to New Zealand from the Pitt Rivers Museum in Oxford, marking a poignant moment of cultural return. This precious cloak was originally presented as a farewell gift by Māori leaders in 1900 to Dr. George Cleghorn, a medical doctor who had worked in New Zealand, and was subsequently loaned to the British museum in 1913 by his widow. The Homebound Project, a dedicated charity, successfully funded the artifact's return, underscoring its profound cultural importance and the desire for ancestral treasures to reside in their homeland. This repatriation symbolizes a growing global recognition of indigenous heritage and the ongoing efforts towards cultural reconciliation.
