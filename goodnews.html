Strong El Niño Leads to Record-Low Atlantic Hurricane Season Activity

2026-09-12 11:26:46 EDT

The Atlantic basin is experiencing an unprecedentedly quiet hurricane season, with no hurricanes formed yet, largely attributed to a powerful El Niño phenomenon. This natural and temporary warming of the equatorial Pacific generates strong wind shear, dry air, and downward-pushing winds that effectively suppress tropical storm formation in the Atlantic. Despite record-warm ocean waters globally, El Niño's atmospheric effects are dominating, making this the lowest activity since the satellite era began in the 1950s. Conversely, the Pacific has seen significantly heightened tropical cyclone activity, and experts warn that next year's Atlantic season could be exceptionally busy as El Niño dissipates.
