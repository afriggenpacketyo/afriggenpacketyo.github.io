2026-11-24 17:34:00 EST

The European Space Agency's Euclid telescope has made a groundbreaking discovery, identifying the most distant known quasar, formally named J1504+6252 and nicknamed Dún Aonghas. This ancient object formed within a mere 630 million years of the universe-creating Big Bang, representing a remarkably early phase in cosmic history. This article explains that quasars are exceptionally bright cores of active galaxies, energized by gas falling into supermassive black holes, and can outshine their host galaxies by thousands of times. The finding provides strong evidence that supermassive black holes grew to immense sizes very quickly in the early universe, supporting theories of direct collapse from large gas clouds. Studying such distant quasars offers invaluable insights into the formation and evolution of the first galaxies.
