Understanding Ranked Choice Voting as Two States Utilize System in Midterms

2026-11-03 20:46:42 EST

This article explains Ranked Choice Voting (RCV), a system used in congressional and state races in Maine and Alaska, where voters rank candidates by preference. If no candidate secures a majority of first-choice votes, the candidate with the fewest is eliminated, and their votes are reallocated based on second choices until a majority winner emerges. Proponents argue RCV diminishes the "spoiler" effect of third-party candidates and encourages more competitive, less negative campaigns, leading to winners with broader support. However, opposition from elected officials has led 19 states to ban the practice, with debates continuing over its effectiveness and learning curve amidst political polarization and misinformation.
